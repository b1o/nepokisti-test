Chapter 6: Pick a Card

Screams from the East

For the first time in almost a week, the Compass Gang had slept in warm beds.

Randall's motel was nothing grand. It had narrow rooms, thin blankets, and a draught under the door. After the road, after Stonecrack and the spiders, it had felt like a palace. The evening before had been full of talk. Professor Derek Fauner and Dr. Philip Flane had told them about their expedition to the old ruins of Alduin. Then Mayor Roartek had come, worried and polite, to explain that for several days huge burrowing bugs called ankhegs had been harassing the village. The party had offered to help. The mayor had accepted gratefully, and they had all gone to bed thinking the problem could wait until morning.

It did not wait.

The screams came from the east just after dawn. They were the high, broken screams of people who had seen something they did not understand. The four of them were out of the motel before they were fully awake. Melmundo ducked under the doorframe, Rank 5 had his daggers already in his hands, Senko was a small shape at the back, and Eronar was buckling his satchel as he ran with Buzz4o buzzing at his shoulder.

Selki was coming apart around them. Most of the villagers ran the other way, into their houses and behind shutters. A few braver ones ran toward the noise with shovels and pickaxes. The party followed them past the last houses to one of the eastern farms, where a fence of grey posts enclosed a field of churned earth.

Mayor Roartek was already there, talking urgently with a few farmhands. A row of curious children stood along the side of the field, too fascinated to be afraid. Inside the fence a merchant was holding off a monster with a long pole.

The monster was the size of a horse and armoured like a beetle. Its mandibles dripped. Each time it lunged, the merchant caught it on the end of his pole and shoved it back, gasping. Beside him an armoured fighter with a sword and a heavy shield stood fast. A little further off, a bard was clapping his hands and shouting encouragement as if he were watching a tournament.

The earth heaved, and more of the creatures came up out of it.

Acid and Armour

The fighters jumped the fence.

Eronar did not jump far. He stopped at the fence line, raised his hands, and the air in front of him folded into a shape: a small ballista of pale blue force, its string drawn and humming. It loosed a bolt into the nearest ankheg and cracked its plating. The creature shrieked. A second ankheg sank back into the ground like a stone into water.

A moment later the ground beside Eronar burst open. A third ankheg came up right next to him and closed its mandibles on his shoulder. Acid hissed into his coat and his skin. The creature gripped him and would not let go.

The armoured fighter took a bite on the shield and did not give an inch. Then the sword came round wrapped in thunder, and the blow knocked the ankheg back across the furrows. The bard clapped.

"Wonderful, darling!" he cried. "Keep going, keep going!"

Buzz4o dived at the ankheg that held his master and stung it, but the armour was too thick and the sting slid off. Melmundo's rage came over him like weather. He roared and swung, and the ankheg turned the blow aside on its plating.

Senko stood back from the fight. She was silent, as she always was among strangers. Light gathered in her hands, and a blessing settled over Eronar, Melmundo and Rank 5, steadying their hands and their hearts. A moment later an ankheg's mandibles caught her too. By luck or by stubbornness she held on to the blessing, and it did not break.

Rank 5 vaulted the fence.

"Friends, hold on," he called. "I'll help you."

He came in low, put his dagger between an ankheg's ribs, and was out of reach again before the creature could turn.

Eronar was still in the jaws of his ankheg. Through the pain, the archivist in him was turning pages. He knew these creatures from the books, and he remembered something.

"They spit!" he shouted. "A line of acid, thirty feet, and it comes back after a while. And they feel the ground. They feel us move. Hiding won't help!"

Tremorsense: the ankhegs felt every footstep through the earth. They could not be crept up on. When Senko's hands began to shape something dark, Eronar saw it and shook his head.

"Senko. If you mean to cast Darkness, there's no point."

So she called up fire instead. It was blue fire, cold-looking and very hot, and it washed over an ankheg's head and set the joints of its armour smoking.

The bard had moved to the side. Now he clapped toward Eronar.

"Come on, boys, faster, faster! These bugs won't beat themselves!"

The armoured fighter was caught too. An ankheg bit through the gap at the knee and grappled, and acid ate into the steel. The shield was blistered and pitted now. Still the fighter held.

Rank 5 had been watching for his moment. He spun both daggers in his hands like a gunslinger showing off at a fair, then drove them down into the joints of an ankheg's shoulders. Both of its forelegs fell to the ground. The creature reeled, and Eronar's ballista put a bolt through one arm of another ankheg and tore it clean away. A fizzing barrier of force flared in front of Eronar just in time, and a bite that should have taken his face closed on air.

The maimed ankheg tried to flee. It turned toward its hole, and the next blow tore its head from its body. It lay still in the furrow.

Then the ground began to bubble.

Larvae came up out of the soil. They were pale, soft things, each the size of a dog, with small mandibles and swollen throats. They spat. Acid struck Melmundo, and one larva sprayed toward the edge of the field, where farmhands and the mayor himself stood. They threw themselves back from the fence. Two more larvae wriggled up from the earth.

Buzz4o tried again. This time his claws found a seam in the ankheg's armour, and his poison went in. Purple pulsed through the creature's veins like dye through water. It shuddered and opened its jaws, and Eronar dropped free, gasping, his shoulder smoking.

Melmundo did not hold anything back. His great blade went through the ankheg's legs on one side, and half of them flew off. The creature dragged itself on in a broken circle, barely alive.

The armoured fighter was in a bad state, burned by acid and bleeding at the joints.

"Hang in there, princess," Rank 5 said.

The fighter did not answer. A gauntleted hand pressed against the worst of the wounds, and a warm light passed from the palm into the flesh. The wounds closed. A moment later the blade glowed with radiant power, and the fighter stepped back into the line.

Senko climbed the fence and stood on top of it, balanced like a cat, looking down at the field. She chose the spot carefully, so that her friends were outside it. Then she struck. The air broke with a clap of thunder, and the blue flame went with it. The burning ankheg was caught in the wave and turned to ash on its feet. The larvae were flung back fifteen feet across the soil, all except one, which dug its claws into the earth and held.

Eronar's ballista swung toward a small larva and fired. The larva burst, and its remains spattered Senko and Rank 5. A spark of fire from Eronar's fingers followed at another one.

The Boy in the White Shirt

The larvae were stubborn. One of them came up under Rank 5, bit his knee, and clung there, its little mandibles working. He swore at it and twisted, but it held fast.

Buzz4o came down on it from above. One sting, deep and true, and the larva went limp.

Then something none of them had cast struck the field. A spell came from somewhere above and behind them, and two of the remaining larvae popped like blisters. The field was quiet.

Eronar turned. At the edge of the field, among the watching children, stood a boy in a white shirt who looked very pleased with himself. Eronar beckoned him over.

"What did you do back there?" he asked. "Congratulations. And why did you do it?"

The boy snapped his fingers. A small glowing orb of light bloomed above his hand.

"I used a little magic to help," he said. "I'm from the village! Nice to meet you! My name's Nigen."

Eronar asked about his friends, who were hanging back and staring.

"They're not as important as me," Nigen said cheerfully. "They don't need names."

When Eronar asked who else in Selki knew magic like his, Nigen said there was only one other: Sky, who was very good at it. Then he remembered that his mother had told him to come home for breakfast. Eronar could find him on the streets any time, he said. Eronar pressed five gold coins into his hand. Nigen's eyes went wide, and he and the other children were off at once, running toward the nearest shop.

Mayor Roartek came across the field. He was pale. One of the larvae had nearly spat on him, he said, and it was the first time he had ever seen ankhegs so close. Melmundo spoke with him, and the mayor's gratitude was plain.

"You can stay as long as you like," he said. "All services will be free for you, food and drink included, for as long as you are in the village."

Horses were another matter, he added. For those they would have to talk to the man who ran the stables.

The Triton's Gifts

They harvested what they could. With Rank 5's help they cut an intact claw from one of the dead ankhegs, twelve pounds of curved, hard chitin. They also drew off a dose of acid, enough to coat one weapon. For now only Eronar knew how to handle it safely. The larvae had no grown acid sacs, so there was nothing to take from them. When the party looked down into the holes, they found hatched eggs at the bottom. The holes went thirty or forty feet down, linked by collapsed tunnels under the farm. It had been a lair, with food above and young below.

Rank 5 was poking about in a broken ankheg arm when his fingers closed on something soft. It was a small coin purse with fifteen gold inside. Some earlier victim must have carried it. He and Melmundo then drew off a second vial of acid.

The merchant with the pole turned out to be a triton, blue-grey and finned, with the easy manner of a man who has talked his way across a continent. He had nearly died in that field and he knew it.

"I travel, after all," he said, when Rank 5 introduced himself. "I'm a trader. A trader of antiques."

His name was Shtab. He had come to Selki to sell things from a new apartment in a new village, and the armoured fighter was his bodyguard. Senko, meeting him, hid half behind Melmundo and said nothing at all. Melmundo introduced them as the Compass Gang.

Shtab gave them two gifts as thanks: a pair of goggles called the Eyes of the Eagle, which sharpened the sight, and a golden Ring of Jumping. Then he showed them a small box.

"I wouldn't give the box away," he said. "It's a keepsake from my father. But I'd part with it if you do me a favour."

It was a Folding Boat, he explained. It had three command words. One made a boat for four, another made a ship for fifteen, and the third folded it back into a box. If the boat was ever destroyed, it became a box again and could not be used until it was mended.

The favour was about his daughter.

He had seen the badge on Eronar's coat. It was the badge of the Dojo, and that was where his daughter worked. Her name was Nami. She had quarrelled with him and run away, and he had not seen her for about five years. His family came from Fort Dokovo in the west. He took a small silver ring from his pocket. It had been his late wife's, and she had wanted Nami to have it when she came of age.

"So you'd do me a huge favour," he said, "if you found her and gave her the ring."

Eronar knew Nami. She held a high rank in the Dojo, he said, and she was very skilled. He thought of her as a mentor. She was somewhere near the ruins now. Shtab looked at him for a long moment, then put the ring into Melmundo's big hand.

He settled one more debt while he was at it, fifty gold he owed from an earlier deal.

"Wherever there's music and drink, you'll find me," he said. "I'm a bard by trade."

Eronar turned to the bodyguard, who was checking the ruined shield and wincing. They talked. The bodyguard's name was Q, and Q came from far away, from Plaza, the capital, a big city that Eronar had never seen.

"I didn't have much left," Q said, "and I needed to earn a living somehow."

Guarding Shtab had been the best work available. That work was nearly over now, and there was none after it. When Eronar asked whether Q would travel with them, Q agreed. The others accepted Q as a temporary member of the Compass Gang. Only then did they notice the badge on the armour. It belonged to Kinleor, the elite division of the Wolves from the capital, whose frontliners were among the empire's main defences. All of them had heard of it.

Meanwhile Shtab had opened another box, a mystery box. For a price you put your hand in and took whatever came out, and then you could buy it. Eronar pulled out a fragrant flower that would sharpen the sense of smell for a moment before it wilted. Shtab wanted twenty gold for it, and Eronar gave it back. Q drew a pair of fancy embroidered ball shoes, worth perhaps ten gold, not magic in the least, and returned them with a grimace. Melmundo drew a small purple book stamped with a flame. It held a firebolt that its reader could call once between rests. Shtab offered it to him for eighty gold, because, he said, he liked Melmundo's beard.

Then Senko stepped up. Behind everyone's back, a small chittering scrape sounded, like a young ankheg stirring in the earth. Every head turned. Shtab jumped. When they looked back, Senko was drawing her hand out of the box. Shtab blinked at her, startled, and saw nothing. Nobody saw the Greater Healing Potion vanish into her sleeve, and nobody was asked to pay for it.

The lucky dips went on. One of them produced a small, ugly goblin head. It did not seem magical, and Shtab had no idea it had been in the box. He offered five gold to have it back. Rank 5 put in his hand and pulled out a bag a little bigger than an ordinary one. It turned out that whoever carried it could carry five times as much.

"Oh, that's a good item," said Shtab. "I'll give you a solid hundred and twenty gold for it."

Rank 5 kept it.

Shtab had been annoying the mayor all morning, and he climbed onto the mayor's cart and rode off with him toward the village. The Compass Gang stood in the ruined field. Their numbers had grown, and so had their pockets. They decided to visit the blacksmith first and the goblin trader after.

The Forge and the Goblin

Buzz4o was put away for the visit. Light flickered inside the small smithy, and a hammer was ringing. The door swung open by itself as they reached it.

The smith was a woman with tall, sharply pointed ears. Eronar knew at once that only high elves had ears like that.

"I'm Blacksmith June," she said, wiping her hands. "I look after most of the gear of those silly guards outside."

"I'm Melmundo," said Melmundo. "This is Q next to me. Behind me is Senko, the little fox. We're the Compass Gang." He thought for a moment. "Or the Habibi Gang."

June specialised in keen weapons. She sharpened and shaped a blade until it bit harder than any ordinary steel. She took no more than two orders at a time. When they laid an ankheg mandible on her bench, she halved her price, because they had brought her the material. She could make a greatsword from it with the acid still in its bones, so that it would burn as it cut. Fifteen gold for the sword and seventy-five to make it keen: ninety in all. Melmundo paid.

"Oh, so you're the ones who dealt with those bugs tormenting the town?" June said. When they said yes, she dropped the price of sharpening a longsword from seventy-five gold to fifty. Both orders would be ready in four days. Melmundo handed his old sword to Q, who weighed it in one hand and nodded.

As they were leaving, June mentioned someone else they might find useful, or dangerous: a shady type called Mishok, who hung around the tavern.

At twenty-five past eight they reached Ardeo's shop. It sold everything: hooks, potions, clothes, jewellery, crystals, books. A staircase led to a second floor. Ardeo was a goblin with a small beard, and when they came in he was arguing with one of several guards over the price of a ball.

"Either you buy," he told the guard, "or you get lost."

His daggers were the sharpest anywhere, he assured them, though not magical. He had two healing potions at fifty gold, one greater healing potion at a hundred, two mana potions at eighty, and one enlarge potion at four hundred and fifty. His crystals came small, cracked, or large and whole. A large red one, a ruby, went to the party for a hundred gold.

Senko said nothing to him at all. She stood in front of the jewellery case and stared. There was a silver ring, a gold ring, earrings, a necklace and a bracelet, and Ardeo said each of them could hold one enchantment. An arcanist could do it, or an enchanter. Eronar explained that his own infusions were not the same thing as enchantments. The party bought the silver ring for ten gold.

Melmundo tried to talk the goblin's prices down. Ardeo listened to the whole speech, then gave him one gold coin to buy himself a drink.

Then Ardeo reached under the table and brought out a deck of cards. They were old and heavy, and the backs were patterned in a way that seemed to move a little when you were not looking at them.

"When you draw a card," he said, "something happens."

Rank 5 wanted to experiment. He paid a hundred gold for the deck.

Q's shield had been eaten by the ankheg acid. Ardeo looked at it, asked eighty gold for a bigger, better one, and finally took the ruined shield and fifty gold in exchange. Three books changed hands for twelve gold together. One was a primer in Undercommon, and another was a tale called The Three Heroes. Ardeo also showed them small blank dolls.

"They're dolls," he said. "They're not magical."

But he explained how voodoo worked: a skilled hand could bind a target's soul to a blank doll.

Rank 5 sold his sapphire for eighty-five gold, and Ardeo set it in the case where the ruby had been. The single greater healing potion went for a hundred. Rank 5 bought the enlarge potion and a healing potion for five hundred gold together, which Ardeo said was a bargain, since the enlarge potion was normally worth nearly eight hundred. At some point Senko sent a whisper of Message to the others: she had given the signal.

The Captain and the Dagger

On their way through the village they stopped at the statue of Kevdar, the god of most dwarves, of smiths and of work, whom many working folk worshipped. The inscription said that something special happened on the tenth of every month. They agreed they ought to come back then.

At a quarter past ten they walked toward the armory. A child had been following them for a while, slipping from doorway to doorway. When they turned, the child ducked behind a statue and was gone.

The armory was a big warehouse. Guards went in and out, lining swords up in racks. The man who came to meet them was an older tiefling, red-skinned, with a face full of old scars.

"Good morning! Welcome to the armory of Selki. How can I help you?"

Melmundo introduced the Compass Gang and asked about replacement armour. The tiefling shook his head. This was not a shop, he said. It was the village store, the guards' armoury and their barracks. He might trade a worn piece of armour for a sound one, but that was all. He was Captain Teor, head of the guards. Senko was introduced as the little fox who did not speak.

Q stepped forward and showed him the Kinleor badge. Teor looked at it for a long time. He had not seen one in years, he said.

"I left the Wolves long ago. I'm a former captain of the Wolves."

There had been too much corruption, he said, and he had walked away from it.

Melmundo said that they had drunk with Ivak. Teor's face changed. Ivak was alive? He had not known, and he was surprised and plainly glad. Ivak was a drider, he said, a drow who had been turned into something like a spider. The party told him that they had made a deal with Ivak to explore a cave together.

"He and I are old friends," Teor said. "I saved his life and he saved mine."

Years ago Ivak had given him a dagger in thanks, and Teor had given Ivak a golden sword in return. Teor took the dagger down now. It was sharper than any ordinary blade. Rank 5 admired it openly and asked if he could have it. Teor thought about it. Ivak trusted these people, so he gave it to him.

"Use the dagger well," he said. "Be careful. It's sharper than normal."

Rank 5 flipped it once and caught it.

"Don't worry. I'll use it properly."

Teor would not come with them. His duty was to the village. But he asked them to carry a message: tell Ivak he was sorry. He had once promised Ivak a place near the village, and he could not keep that promise, because most people would never accept him. He laughed, a little ruefully, about the first time he had tried to pet Ivak's spiders. They had attacked him, he had almost died, and his arm had nearly come off.

Eronar asked about Kaguya. Her followers had caused them trouble a few weeks before.

"The Black Goddess," Teor said. "She controls illusions. Most of her followers are fanatics. They would die in her name."

They were talented in forbidden magic, he said: blood magic, shadow magic, water magic. If the party had to fight them, they should use radiant damage.

Q asked about the corruption in the Wolves. It had grown a great deal, Teor said. Since the old emperor died, most of the Wolves had lost interest in the new one, whom they thought weak and incapable. According to the newspaper, the emperor had recently visited the continent, and the city of Tom and Jerry. Rank 5 mentioned that he was from Tom and Jerry, and Teor nodded: the emperor had been there for the birthday of the Yarl's little daughter, Yara or some such name. He warned them not to go south to the Kingdom of Bulgaria, whose people did not like the new empire.

Things in the east were worse. Goblins and hobgoblins were rising again, and they seemed to be allies now. Rumour said they were kidnapping people, mostly around Kifi, Mrats and Tom and Jerry. The party exchanged looks. They thought they knew what became of people who were taken like that. They were turned into something else.

Then Teor told them about the cave.

Years ago his battalion had been chased by goblins and hobgoblins, and he had been cut off from the others. In the confusion he had fallen into a cave called Pueven, and he had landed in a web. Ivak had found him there. They had protected each other under a rain of fire arrows and escaped together.

They asked him about Sky. Teor did not know him well, he said, and they only greeted each other. Sky was a good, hard-working boy, but very secretive and strange, and he talked to few people. About a month ago he had opened the tavern with his brother and sister. He had come from Tom and Jerry, where he had owned a house.

The Missing Boy

Rank 5 had not forgotten the child who had followed them. He wanted to find the boy before they left Selki. He was sure the boy was hiding something.

They asked around. A village boy said that he had last seen Nigen with a ball, together with another boy, Raicho. The two of them had gone toward the forest about an hour ago and had not come back. The children played hide and seek there, he said.

"We met a bear once," he added proudly. "We threw stones at it and it chased us, but we got away."

He got a few copper coins for his trouble and ran off.

They were tired and still burned from the fight. They sat down on some benches for an hour's rest. Eronar worked at a little smithing while they talked over the new treasures. The Ring of Jumping suited someone who fought up close and had nothing to throw, so they gave it to Q. The Eyes of the Eagle turned out to need no attunement, and they were offered to Melmundo or Senko. Senko sent her opinion quietly through Message: keep the magic things. Don't sell them.

The Raven One

At twenty to twelve they went to the tavern called the Raven One. It was full, and there was no music.

Rank 5 asked for Mishok, and he found him. Rank 5 was not polite about it, and he pushed. Mishok stood up, and his coat fell open on a pair of daggers. For a moment the room held its breath. Then Melmundo took each of them by the collar, one in each hand, and held them apart like a man separating two dogs.

"You're playing a very dangerous game," Mishok said.

But business was business. He sold poison at a hundred gold a vial. He handed Rank 5 one vial of Assassin's Blood, enough to coat a dagger for a minute, and then two more. When the deal was done, he straightened his collar.

"You've treated the only fence in this area terribly," he said.

He turned his back on them, opened the door, and was gone.

Rank 5 bought four beers for the table. Eronar laid the deck of cards out and cast Identify on it. What came back made him go quiet. There was something very good in the cards, and something very bad. One card might give a magic weapon. Another might trap a soul in a crystal on another plane of existence. It was a Deck of Many Things.

They talked about it for a long time.

Someone handed Senko a colouring book. Blue fire flickered at her fingers. The water in the cup she held boiled away, and the book burned to ash in her hands.

Melmundo asked Senko to take his potriffon, Heritobibubka, for a walk. Out behind the tavern Heritobibubka sat by the cart. She had not eaten for days, and she had always liked Senko more than anyone. Senko fed her, slowly, and something passed between them: trust, and a kind of bond. The potriffon could bond with two creatures at most. When she was close to Senko, Senko felt steadier, harder to shake.

They ordered food. Senko went behind the cart, and where a quiet girl had been there was now a fox with too many tails. She played with the potriffon in the dust. The bond between them grew deeper, and Heritobibubka trusted her more than ever.

So Senko was not at the table when they drew the cards.

The Vizier and the Donjon

Rank 5 drew first. The card showed a robed figure with knowing eyes. The Vizier. Any time within the next year, he could ask a single question and receive a true answer.

Eronar looked at the deck for a long moment. Then he spoke to the others without looking up.

"If I disappear, or something else happens to me," he said, "Buzz4o goes to Senko. And tell her I'm sorry."

He drew.

The card was dark, with a cell door painted on it. The Donjon.

There was no sound and no flash. Eronar was simply gone. His clothes, his tools, his satchel and everything he carried fell to the floor where he had sat, as if the man inside them had been pulled out through a gap in the world.

Far away, somewhere dark, a blue crystal sphere hung in the blackness. Inside it Eronar lay frozen, without his gear, unable to move, waiting to be found.

The table was silent. Then they started to move. He was not dead, they understood. His Bag of Holding was still active, and so were the infusions he had worked into their gear. Rank 5 knelt and gathered everything Eronar had dropped into the bag, and he kept it.

Melmundo went out behind the cart. He was frightened, and it showed in his face. He told Senko that Eronar had vanished, and that Buzz4o was hers now, because that was what Eronar had wanted.

Senko did not believe them. She looked at each of them in turn, searching their faces for the lie, the way she had searched so many faces before. There was no lie.

They went back inside. Rank 5 sat, closed his eyes, and reached for the gift the Vizier had given him.

"I ask, deep within myself," he said. "Eronar vanished in front of us. How do we bring him back?"

Letters flashed behind his eyes, burning and clear.

You must save him from the cave he is in. Around him are many undead, and his time is running out.

A cave full of undead. They all remembered at once. Ivak's friend had spoken of a cave with a necromancer in it, and of the dead. Pueven. The same cave where Teor had once fallen into a web.

They searched the room. Under a chair they found a small blue crystal with the number 1 on it. It was a key, the first of several, to a larger crystal. Some spells needed keys like that to be unlocked.

When they left, the cards were still lying on the table.

A Horse Called Gabriel

The stables belonged to a ratfolk called Pil. He listed his horses for them. The best was a large warhorse with a speed mutation, which covered seventy feet at a stride and cost four hundred and fifteen gold. Melmundo argued that they needed something strong. One horse would have to carry him, and later Eronar too.

Pil listened to why they were in such a hurry.

"I can just give you a horse," he said, "on your promise to return it afterward."

The horse was Gabriel, his fastest. They hitched him to their cart.

"Just," Pil said, watching them go, "I hope you bring him back."

Ost Taker

Ost Taker was four hours from Selki, or three with Gabriel pulling. They arrived at a quarter past two.

Austin was waiting there with a horse, his arms crossed.

He told them his news without wasting words. Two days ago he had escaped Drisko's camp. He had intercepted the cart that was carrying slaves to the other camp, killed every one of Drisko's men with it, and freed the slaves, who had gone west. He had carried Asen's body into a nearby cave and burned it. From Asen's pockets he had taken a small bag, and he gave it to Rank 5: two red fire runes, a healing potion and a vial of basic poison.

Then he took out Drisko's sending stone, crushed it in his fist, and threw the pieces into the valley.

He asked where Eronar was. They told him.

"You're idiots," said Austin.

He told them to give the cards to the Archive, because the Archive would know what to do with them. When they said they were going to Pueven cave, he shook his head. He would not come. His father Dortan was in the village, and Austin meant to take him to the docks and leave the continent. Drisko knew what had happened now, he said. Drisko would hunt Austin, and he would hunt them. Beating him was impossible. He had too many connections and too much power.

Melmundo wondered aloud whether Maverick might have more power than Drisko. Maybe, Austin allowed. He still would not advise trying.

"If you want to kill yourselves, go. But not me." He looked at them hard. "Is it really necessary to save someone like him?"

"We have no choice," Melmundo said. "However much you dislike him, he's one of us. And he isn't dead, it seems."

Austin was quiet for a moment. Then he held out his hand, and Melmundo moved as if to pull him into an embrace.

"I don't hug," said Austin.

He shook Melmundo's hand instead and pressed a stone into it.

"This sending stone is tuned to me. Once a day you can reach me."

Then he mounted his horse and rode away. He did not look back.

Five and a Half Days
