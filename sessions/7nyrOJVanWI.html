Chapter 2: Feathers and Blood

Morning at the Dris Tavern

Senko woke first. She always did. Something in her kept time better than any bell, and every morning she opened her eyes at the same hour, whatever the night had been like. Grey light came through the shutters of the Dris Tavern. The room smelled of old ale and woodsmoke, and of the sleepers around her.

Eronar woke soon after her. He sat up, put his spectacles on, and checked by habit that the Archive badge was still pinned to his coat.

Heritobibubka was the next to wake. Melmundo's Potriffon stretched in her corner until her long neck cracked, then each leg in turn. She looked around the room for something to do and found Rank 5. The tiefling was still asleep, one hand closed around the gem he never put down, even in sleep. She flapped once, twice, and landed on his forehead.

Rank 5 woke with a curse. He had slept badly since the night in the ritual room, when he had stepped through the portal of blood. His dreams had been full of things he could not name.

Melmundo laughed, lifted Heritobibubka off his friend, and took her outside for her morning walk. The big aasimar spoke softly to her as they went around the yard. She pressed her head against his palm, and he took it as a good sign.

When they came back in, two beds were empty. Kollioh and Essail had gone. The apeling's blankets were folded and the yuan-ti's pack was missing.

Eronar did not say anything about it yet. He was looking at Rank 5. In the daylight the veins under the tiefling's eyes stood out dark and swollen, and the veins along his forearms did too, as if something under the skin were pushing to get out. They had not looked like that before the temple.

"Hold still," Eronar said. He took Rank 5's wrist, turned his arm to the light, pressed a thumb under his eye and looked closely. He was a historian before he was a healer, but he knew a good deal about bodies. Still, he found nothing he could name. No fever, no poison he knew, no wound. The veins were simply more than before. He let go of the arm and frowned, and he remembered it.

Downstairs the bartender announced the morning's dish with some pride: tripe soup.

"Tripe soup," Rank 5 said. "Only masochists eat that."

The bartender looked hurt, as a man does when his cooking is insulted in his own house. Melmundo stepped in before things got worse. "Breakfast for everyone," he said, and put his coin on the bar. "On me. My birthday's tomorrow."

Four bowls came, with bread and a saucer of crumbs for the animals. Heritobibubka and Eronar's Buzzarect, Buzz4o, shared the crumbs on the table and fought over the bigger ones. The bartender charged twenty-one silver for all of it, and Melmundo paid without arguing.

They talked over the steam from the bowls. Rank 5 said quietly that he came from the Tom and Jerry, and that their new employer did not sit well with him. "Drisko's suspicious," he said. "I don't like how all that ended."

"Then let's see what he has for us before we tie ourselves to him," Melmundo said. "The harpy business first. Then we decide."

Austin in Drisko's Chair

At the back of the tavern, outside Drisko's office, the blue dragonborn Austin stood talking with Asen. The tiefling's skin was a cold blue too, and the two of them were clearly not enjoying the talk. When the party came near, Asen smiled. It was a smooth, oily smile.

"Well, we'll leave you to it," he said to Austin. "I suppose you'll handle things here, right?"

On his way past he patted Eronar on the shoulder, as if they were old friends, and walked out of the tavern. Austin watched him go with a look that could have cut stone.

Drisko was not in the office. Austin went in, sat down in Drisko's chair without asking anyone, and waved the party to the seats.

"The boss has gone west," he said. "Something important. So you get me."

Then he told them the job. There was an iron-ore camp three hours south, near Pueven cave, called Stonecrack. It took raw ore from the mine and sent the processed iron to towns and villages across the region, and a big part of the Kartella's money came from it. Harpies had been attacking it. They stole, they stopped the work, and they killed. Twelve people were dead in three days.

"Four hundred and eighty gold," Austin said. "And five gold for every harpy feather you bring me."

He reached into a drawer and tossed something onto the desk in front of them: a flat disc of brass and stone with a thin blade standing up from its face. Melmundo and Senko looked at it blankly. Rank 5 and Eronar knew what it was at once. It was a sundial, a rare and expensive thing. Eronar picked it up.

"What exactly is your connection to Drisko?" he asked.

Austin was silent for a while. "He helped me. He pulled me out of the mud. Without him I'd probably be dead." He turned one big hand over on the desk. "I owe him a lot. That doesn't mean I agree with him. Often I don't."

"And the workers at this camp?"

"Slaves." Austin said it plainly. "They can't leave. They're not allowed to." He looked at the window, though there was nothing to see through it but the yard. "I was part of that camp. But I left. Because I am not weak."

Nobody in the room missed what he did not say. Drisko, by Austin's own account, did not care much about Stonecrack. Austin did.

Eronar asked where Kollioh and Essail had gone. Austin said he had sent them west to guard Drisko for a few days. "One can talk, the other can fight," he said. "That's a good pair." He had taken the healing potion the apeling had been carrying, and now he put it on the desk next to the sundial. "That's yours. He won't need it where he is."

Rank 5 took it and put it away for the fight to come.

Eronar asked what had happened to Rank 5 in the ritual chamber, and what the shadow had been, the one that had dragged Mabus Bur into the earth. Austin shook his head.

"I don't know. I don't know rituals." He spread his scarred hands. "I can hit things. Very hard. I can kill people. Very brutally. That's what I know." His eyes went to Melmundo's chest, where the medallion of Hikari hung. "That light of yours. What was it?"

"A mystery to me too," Melmundo said honestly. After a moment he added that he thought it came from his goddess, and from the temple itself. Austin nodded slowly. Around his own neck a silver dragon's head caught the light: the sign of Bahamut, whose paladin he was.

Something moved near the door. Austin raised his voice without turning around. "Pesho. Don't eavesdrop." There was a scuffle of retreating feet.

"There's a cart waiting outside," Austin told them. "Take it. Go and look at the camp."

What Senko Heard

Back at their table, the party talked in low voices. Melmundo and Eronar both knew by now that Senko could hear a lie the way other people hear a wrong note. While the others talked, a whisper reached Eronar that only he could hear. It was the kitsune's voice, carried on her small magic.

He's not lying. Austin's honest.

Eronar gave the smallest nod.

The talk turned to what they knew about harpies. They looked for food and shelter. They struck first. Their songs could charm a creature and make it walk toward them. Melmundo leaned back and said, as if it were nothing, "Well, I can talk with all the creatures I want to talk with." When the others stared at him, he explained that he spoke Celestial. So did Senko. Eronar took out his notebook and wrote it down carefully.

They agreed to go and see the camp for themselves.

The Old Road

The cart was waiting in the yard as promised, and a sturdy horse named Vihar stood in the traces. The road ran south through rolling country under a high, pale sky. Three hours, Austin had said, and the sundial told them how the hours passed.

Eronar did not waste them. He sat in the back of the cart with his tools spread on a cloth and worked his artifice into their weapons, one after another. Melmundo's longsword came first. When Eronar handed it back, the blade hummed faintly, truer and keener than before. Then he did one of Rank 5's two daggers, and then his own.

After a while the road became older. The stones were sunk deeper and the ruts were worn by wheels from another age. Then Vihar shied, and they saw why.

A cart lay smashed across the old road. It was not just overturned but torn apart. Its boards were split, and huge claw marks were raked through the wood as if something had tried to open it like a shell. Heritobibubka took off from Melmundo's shoulder and circled the wreck with sharp cries. Melmundo and Senko searched the rocks around it. There was blood on the stones, dark and dry, days old. Someone had been ambushed here.

Senko bent and picked something out of the dust. It was a feather, broken and cut, purple along its length and red at the tip. It was a harpy's feather.

Eronar climbed into what was left of the cart. Inside, on a board that had survived, someone had cut two letters: D.F.

He sat back on his heels. "Derek Fauner."

The others waited.

"A dwarf. Very old. A historian, an archaeologist, the best there is. He found a Titan fossil in the forest of Goren Izmet, a huge one. Every scholar I know respects him." Eronar looked at the claw marks and the blood. "This is his cart. He was attacked."

He turned the harpy feather over in his fingers, but it told him nothing more. He had found the initials of an important man on a wrecked cart, he said. He wanted to know where that man was now, and whether he was still alive.

The Black-Winged Stranger

The bush beside the road shook, and something fell out of it.

At first they saw only wings, black as a crow's and far larger. Then they saw a woman under them, slender, with dark skin and a frightened face. Heritobibubka went wild, screaming and beating her wings. Melmundo caught her and held her close, murmuring until she calmed.

"I have nothing to do with this," the winged woman said quickly, before anyone could ask. "Nothing. I was only passing."

She's lying, came Senko's whisper in Eronar's ear.

The stranger must have seen it in their faces. She threw herself into the air. But her left wing was torn and bleeding, and it gave out under her at once. She tumbled sideways, hit a rock with a crack, and lay still.

Rank 5 got to her first. He caught her limp body before it rolled off the stone. Together he and Melmundo carried her to a large boulder and tied her to it with rope from the cart. While they did, Eronar saw her back. There were long claw marks there, the same as the ones in the cart's boards.

He knelt and laid a hand on her, and the soft light of healing passed from his palm into her. She gasped awake and pulled against the ropes. Then she saw that they were not going to hurt her and went still.

She told them her name was Airiene.

When they asked about the cart, she admitted that she knew it. "It belongs to an old man. A very nice old man." She had come back to save some of his things, she said, and harpies had caught her here.

Truth, Senko whispered to Eronar.

"Harpies did this to you?" Rank 5 asked, nodding at her wing.

"A group of harpies. My kind." Her mouth twisted. "They clearly don't like the ones with black wings."

Buzz4o chose that moment to buzz past her face. Airiene flinched, shrank as far behind the boulder as the ropes allowed, and called it disgusting. Eronar did not take offence for his companion.

Then she told them the rest. She had been a slave for a year in a camp south of here, run by Kartella men. The slaves worked from dawn to dusk. They broke stone, smelted iron ore at a furnace so hot it took the skin off your hands, dug in the mine, and pushed loaded carts uphill. There were things living deep in the mine too, she said, huge and bloodthirsty.

"Who runs it?" Eronar asked.

"Tomon. A terrible man. He is the operator of the camp." Under him, slaves died of exhaustion and thirst, she said, and of whip wounds that went bad in the heat. The guards had killed her friends in front of her.

A few days ago, harpies had flown over the camp. She had called up to them and begged them for help. They had answered by falling on the camp and on her. In the chaos she had broken free of her chains and run.

"There's an old dwarf there," she said. "Kind. He helped me, more than anyone. They call him old Fauner." When she flew away, he had been hiding. She did not know what had happened to him since.

Eronar's face went very still.

Some harpies had strong magic, she warned them. She knew what they could do. Then her voice dropped, and she looked at the road south. She was afraid, but she was also something harder. She wanted the slaves free. She wanted revenge.

"But now," she said. "Right now—" She nodded, as if she had decided something. "I can!"

A Coat for a Harpy

They untied her. If she was coming with them, she could not walk into a Kartella camp with black wings on her back. Rank 5 had the answer. He pulled a folded garment from his pack and put it around her shoulders. It fastened itself as soon as it touched her. The cloth flowed over her wings and closed into a long hooded robe, with a mask that covered the lower half of her face.

"This is a magic Nike Switcher," Rank 5 said proudly. "Tom and Jerry exclusive brand."

Airiene looked down at herself, startled, and moved her hidden wings under the cloth.

Then came the harder question. Eronar asked it, because someone had to. "You all understand what our job actually is? We're being paid to protect that camp. For the Kartella. Our job is to make sure the slaves stay slaves."

"I won't do that," Melmundo said. "Not if I've seen what she described. If it costs us Drisko's gold, then it costs us Drisko's gold."

"If we free them, we're against the Kartella," Eronar said. "All of them."

Rank 5 shrugged. "I don't want to be the righteous hero. I just want gold." He jerked his chin at the wreck. "But a famous dwarf who gets saved might pay very well." He would be the best rogue the Tom and Jerry had ever seen, he added. He would go from rank five to rank one. Saving rich old scholars fit that plan nicely.

Eronar looked at the initials on the board. "I don't much care who I'm with. Whether the Archive or the Kartella. I have my own goal." He wanted Fauner, and what Fauner knew. Somewhere in that old dwarf's memory there might be something about Eronar's own past.

They agreed on one thing. They would look before they did anything else.

Over the Hill

Airiene rode with them in the cart, hooded and silent. The camp stood in a stretch of cleared forest, and they left the cart and Vihar in the hollow below a hill that overlooked it. Melmundo kept Heritobibubka close. A Potriffon flying free over the camp would draw every eye on the walls.

They crept up through the scrub to the top. Eronar found his view blocked by rock and trees no matter where he stood. Melmundo crouched, Senko climbed onto his broad shoulders, and he stood up slowly until she could see over the ridge.

She counted. About ten slaves moved through the yard, bent under loads. There were at least fifteen guards, posted at every point of the camp. Near the back wall sat two slaves apart from the rest, who seemed to be special in some way. A lift went down into a hole in the earth. One of the bandits walked the wall with a bow.

She slid down and told the others, and Melmundo repeated what she said where it was needed.

Plans by the Cart

Back at the cart, they argued in whispers.

"Sneaking in is a bad idea," Airiene said. "They're very watchful. They watch all the time. There are eyes everywhere." She had a better idea. They should walk in openly as help for the camp, deal with the harpies, earn some trust, and then get the slaves out at night.

Eronar had his own idea. He could make an alchemy jug with his artifice and fill it with a basic poison mixed with Fructose, the drug he had bought at the tavern the night they all met. Whoever drank it would be stunned, and there would be enough for every guard in the camp. "We give it to them as a gift," he said. "From Austin."

"From Alastin," Rank 5 said, then caught himself. "My uncle. Alastin's my uncle." Nobody believed him for a moment, and nobody said so.

He recovered and offered to hand out the drink himself. Nobody in the group could talk people around better than he could, he pointed out, and the Tom and Jerry spoke the same language as bandits.

Airiene had one more weapon. Once a day she could sing a song that might charm everyone who heard it within three hundred feet, all the guards at once. The charmed would come to her and surround her, and they would follow her wherever she went for about ten minutes. It would not last long. And she was not sure it would work at all.

Senko had seen a stable inside the walls with three horses in it. With horses and carts they could move the slaves out quickly. Melmundo wanted one thing above everything else. "All of them free. Nobody dies."

In the end they had a plan, or at least the start of one. Austin had sent them. Tomorrow was Melmundo's birthday, which was true, and what was a birthday without a party for their new friends on the walls? The drink would do the rest. That was Plan A. If it failed, Airiene would sing. Eronar unbuckled his watch and gave it to her so she could time the song. She promised to give a sign before she began, so that they could cover their ears.

Everything else they would have to make up as they went.

The Stone Gates

Camp Stonecrack stood behind huge stone gates. Two bandits stood guard there, and one of them sat behind a loaded ballista whose iron head pointed straight at the cart.

"Austin sent us," Eronar called up. "Reinforcements."

The gate opened. Vihar pulled the cart into the yard, and they left it there.

The man who came to meet them had a long beard and the tired, hard look of someone who counts every coin twice. His eyes went straight to the badge on Eronar's coat and stayed there a moment too long. This was Tomon. He had heard nothing about reinforcements from Austin, he said, but he did not send them away.

The camp was everything Airiene had said. The air was thick with dust and the heat of the furnace. Slaves in chains moved between the rock piles and the carts, and some of them were children. Whips cracked. A huge bandit, Tumbo, Tomon's brother, carried a heavy whip and two swords, one of them bigger than Melmundo's. He was beating an old dragonborn slave who was breaking stone and shouting at him with every stroke.

Then, near the furnace, a bandit grabbed a young slave by the collar and threw him to the ground.

"Saboteur!"

"I didn't do it!" the boy cried. "I swear, I didn't—"

The bandit kicked him. Then again, and again. The boy tried to curl up, then stopped trying. The kicking went on after he stopped moving. When the bandit finally stepped back, the boy was dead.

Melmundo's hand had gone to his sword. He did not draw it. It was the hardest thing he had done all day.

Tomon did not even look around. "I suppose you know," he said, as if nothing had happened, "but the biggest iron mine on the damn continent is here."

He listed his troubles as they walked. The lift chain had broken. Somebody had set off a charge of dynamite down below, and the blast had buried most of the mine. Then, four days ago, one harpy had come, a clever, sociable one, and looked the place over. She had called the others. They had attacked every day since. They had killed forty percent of his guards and sixty percent of his slaves. He would have to find more volunteers to replace them, he said, and he did not mean the word kindly.

A woman bandit came up beside him, lean and sharp-eyed. She leaned close to Senko and sniffed her, like a dog checking a stranger.

"Welcome," she said. "I am Grisa. Nice to meet you."

Behind her a bandit had stopped to watch the newcomers. Grisa turned and hit him across the head without looking, and he hurried back to work. She was one of Tomon's hands, and she clearly enjoyed the work.

The party tried their opening, but Tomon turned them down flatly. "You are in a camp whose one purpose is to work and run efficiently," he said. There would be no celebrations and no drinking while the harpies still flew. Kill them first. Then he would drink with them as much as they liked. His money was running out, he admitted, and so were his supplies. A coal delivery was coming tomorrow, and he could not pay for it.

When someone held out a hand to seal the deal, Tomon looked at it coldly. "Not here. Here a handshake is a challenge to a duel." He raised his voice so the whole yard could hear. "And whoever set off that dynamite will hang. I promise you that."

He had started to turn away when his eyes fell on the hooded figure at the back of the group.

"And who is that?"

Airiene's masked face did not move. Tomon stepped closer. For a moment it seemed that the whole plan would fall apart in the dust of the yard, because Rank 5 had begun to say something careless. But the tiefling caught himself and turned the slip into a smooth, easy story. She was their scout, he said, the best tracker they had, quiet and shy with strangers. She would lead them to the harpies.

Tomon looked at the hood a moment longer. Then he grunted and let them go. Airiene had never heard of Austin. She knew only the camp and the people in it, and she had said nothing. It was enough.

The Way to the Nest

Airiene led them west, out of the camp and into the wooded hills. The harpies were not hard to follow once you knew what to look for, and she knew. There were claw scratches on the bark and dried blood on the stones. Feathers had caught in the thorns. They found a hand, half eaten. Further up, a corpse lay draped over a sharp rock, left where it had fallen or been dropped.

The nest was high among the rocks above a wooded slope. Rough bowls of sticks and bones sat on the ledges, and bodies lay all around. Five harpies were there. Some fed on the dead and some slept.

Airiene spoke quietly about them. They respected strength. They hunted like wolves, together, and finished whatever was weakest.

The party spread out and moved forward through the trees and bushes, slowly and carefully. Airiene took to the air on her good wing and dropped into the brush on the far side. They got closer, and closer still.

Then Eronar put his foot down on a dry branch.

The crack echoed off the rocks. Five heads came up at once.

Talons

Rank 5 was already out of sight. Before the first harpy had spread its wings, his daggers came out of the shadows and found it, cutting deep, and he was gone again. Melmundo roared. The rage of his blood took hold of him, and the infused longsword rose in his fists, glowing faintly. Senko wrapped herself in a shimmer of arcane armour. Eronar spoke a word over his pistol, and a sickly green poison spread over the barrel and into every shot.

Airiene burst out of the bushes and shouted at the harpies in a language none of the others knew. Whatever she said hit home. One of them shrieked and dived at her. Airiene met it in midair and dragged it down, and they crashed to the ground together, wrestling.

A harpy swung a spiked club into Rank 5's belly. He doubled over, swearing.

Then the singing began.

It was beautiful, too beautiful, a sweet clear voice that slid into the mind and settled there. Rank 5 lowered his daggers and turned toward the sound with a soft, empty smile. Senko began to walk across the clearing toward the singing harpy, step by step. A talon raked across Rank 5's shoulder, and the pain broke the spell. He staggered back into himself, furious.

Melmundo reached the first harpy he could. The longsword came down in one great stroke, and the creature fell in two halves.

Airiene was losing her fight on the ground. Claws had opened her arms and side, and her black feathers were wet. "Save Airiene!" Rank 5 shouted. Melmundo turned, swung again, and a second harpy died.

A third harpy came down on Rank 5 from above. Its talons closed on his shoulders and lifted him. The ground fell away, ten feet, twenty, thirty, and the harpy let go.

He hit the rocks hard and did not get up.

Senko raised her hand and sent a ray of white frost into the harpy that had dropped him. Ice spread over its wings, and it slowed and dropped lower. Melmundo moved to stand over Rank 5's body, sword up, and struck again at anything that came near.

Rank 5 lay in the dark. Somewhere far off he could still hear the fight. He held on.

Another harpy shrieked, and the song rose again, trying to take hold. Airiene sang back. Her voice cut through the harpy's song, and the charm broke before it could take anyone. One of the harpies circling above was bleeding from below. Melmundo saw it and understood. She had given birth not long ago. She swooped at him, and the medallion of Hikari on his chest blazed. Light gathered around his head like a helmet, and her claws skidded off it harmlessly.

Airiene limped to Rank 5's side and sang to him: a few low notes, a healing word. His eyes opened. He was hurt, barely able to stand, but alive. A healing potion went down his throat after it, and its warmth closed some of the wounds.

The mother harpy turned on Senko and knocked her down. For a moment the kitsune lay still. Then shadows gathered under her, the dark magic in her blood refusing to let her go, and she got up again, swaying, with almost nothing left. Airiene's song reached her too, and some of her strength came back.

Eronar steadied his pistol on his arm and fired. The poisoned shot took a harpy in the chest, and it fell from the sky dead.

The Last Harpy

When the fighting stopped, only the mother was left.

She knew it. She turned and beat her wings hard, heading for the open sky.

"Alive," Eronar said. "I want her alive."

Senko raised her hand one more time. She held back the killing cold this time and sent only enough frost to freeze the wings. Ice cracked across the feathers. The harpy fell out of the air and lay unconscious among the stones.

Airiene was on her before anyone else, her face twisted with hate.

"Wait," Rank 5 said, catching her arm. "She'll get the fate she deserves. But right now we need information that only she can give."

Eronar backed him up. For a long moment Airiene stood trembling between them. Then she stepped back.

They tied the harpy up tightly and healed her just enough to wake her. She came around snarling.

They asked her why the camp. Why the cart. She gave them little. Someone had been seen calling them, she said. At the camp they had found easy targets and plenty of food, so they took it. Nothing more.

They asked about the nest and her young. The harpy looked at them with flat, cold eyes.

"Better that I survive," she said. "I'll make new ones. If these die, they were weak."

Then her gaze slid past them to the hooded figure behind them, and something like contempt crossed her face. She knew what Airiene was. She named it out loud: a Draumar, the rare and hated child of harpy and drow elf.

Airiene stepped forward, grabbed the harpy by the hair, and smashed her head into the rock. Then she did it again. Nobody stopped her. When she let go, the harpy was dead.

Ashes and Feathers

Eronar climbed up to the nests. In the largest one, among the bones and twigs, two small things moved. They were baby harpies, each about the size of an old cat, with stubby wings and wide frightened eyes. He gathered them up and carried them down to the others.

Rank 5 looked at them thoughtfully. "Someone would pay for those."

"No," Eronar said at once, and sharply. "Absolutely not."

Airiene looked at the two of them for a long time. "I won't kill children," she said finally. She could not raise them herself, but she knew someone who could. She held out her arms, and Eronar put the babies in them. One curled at once into the crook of her elbow and pressed against her. The other only stared up at her, unblinking.

Then they took care of the dead. They pulled feathers from the bodies by the handful, three hundred and twenty-five in all, enough at five gold each to make one thousand six hundred and twenty-five gold when Austin paid out. Melmundo still had the one feather from the wrecked cart. Senko looked at it and said through him that it mattered. She thought it might link these harpies to something more. It was a harpy's feather, plain enough. But she kept her eyes on it.

Eronar knelt beside one of the dead harpies, drew his knife, and cut off its head. He wrapped it in cloth to take with them. Austin would want proof, and so might Tomon.

Then they burned the nests. Senko helped them catch, and soon the sticks and bones were burning, and black smoke rose from the rocks into the late sky.

Airiene stood at the edge of the clearing with the two babies held against her chest. She looked south, toward the camp she had escaped.

"Save my friends," she said. "Please."

She went to Rank 5. She sang to him one last time, the same low healing notes, and the last of his wounds closed. Then she put her free arm around him and hugged him hard, and there were tears on her face.

She stepped back, opened her black wings, and rose into the evening air. They watched until she was only a dark speck over the hills, and then she was gone.

The fire crackled behind them. The camp of Stonecrack waited to the south, with its whips, its ballista, its buried mine, and an old dwarf hiding somewhere inside it.

They turned back toward it.
