Chapter 4: One Against All

A Morning Among Spiders

Stonecrack belonged to the spiders now.

Senko woke first, as she always did. The sundial in the yard showed a quarter past six. Grey light spread over the camp that had been Drisko's. Webs hung from the eaves of the barracks, across the doorways and along the fences. Pale threads ran from post to post like rigging on a ship. Ivak's children had been busy in the night. Here and there a long-legged shape moved slowly over the roofs. One of them sat on the gallows where Karpik had been hanged, as if it had always lived there.

Melmundo was still asleep. He was dreaming of a great war: giants walked across the plains and titans broke the sky open, and he stood in front of them with his sword. Heritobibubka lay curled up asleep inside his discarded armor, small and warm.

Senko looked at him for a moment with a fox's smile. She moved her fingers, and a small image of Heri appeared next to the big man's ear and gave a terrible, piercing scream.

Melmundo sat up at once. The giants were gone. His hands were already searching the blankets, the floor and the air around him for his frightened pet, and the illusion faded. The real Heri lifted her head from the armor and blinked at him, offended.

"Good morning, friends!" Rank 5 called. He was already on his feet and stretching, as cheerful as if he had slept in a palace and not in a slavers' camp.

Dortan got up more slowly. Every joint in the old dragonborn's body cracked as he stood, one after another. He looked around at the webs and the spiders and his face showed only one wish. These strangers had promised to take him to his son, and he would rather be with them than anywhere else. He offered to cook breakfast, but he had nothing to cook. The party shared their own food with him, and he ate it gratefully.

Eronar went back to the forge, where he had felt something strange the night before. He spoke the words of his spell and let his senses open. He felt no magic. The cold hearth held no coals, only spider silk, clean and neatly laid. Whatever he had felt in the dark, the spiders had covered it over or it had never been there.

The Sickness

Heri woke unhappy. She crawled out of the armor, walked a few unsteady steps and left green droppings on the ground.

Melmundo stared at them. Eronar knelt beside the little potriffon, half griffon and half cat, and examined her with the care of a man who had read more medical books than most healers ever opened. He felt her throat and listened to her breathing. She was tired, far too tired for an animal that had slept all night.

"It's her throat," he said at last. "An illness of the throat. For her kind it is very dangerous." He looked up at Melmundo. "If a healer doesn't see her, she could die. Within a week, perhaps."

For a moment the giant aasimar said nothing. Then he straightened up, and the decision was plain on his face.

"Heri has to be as well as possible," he said. Nothing mattered more to him now. They would go to the Dris Tavern. Someone there would know a healer.

While the others got ready, Rank 5 tried to make friends with one of the spiders near the gallows. He crouched, held out a hand and spoke softly, the way he would speak to a nervous horse. The spider raised its front legs, and its whole body made it clear that this was its yard and he should leave. Rank 5 left.

Vihar had eaten his hay and was ready. Melmundo put the party's money into the Bag of Holding, Rank 5 climbed up to drive, and the cart rolled out of Stonecrack and down the road toward the tavern.

The Old Man's Story

The road took two hours. Rank 5 held the reins and the others sat in the back. At some point someone said something unkind about the garment Rank 5 was wearing.

"Bro, please don't talk about my switcher like that," Rank 5 said, hurt. "I paid a lot for it. And it's not a rag."

Eronar wrote something in his notebook, where he kept the things he did not want to forget. Senko pulled a cover over Dortan in the back of the cart. If anyone looked, they would see a bundle and nothing more. The old man lay very still and played dead well.

He could not keep quiet for long, though. Under the cover and later beside it, he told them about his family as the cart rocked along. He had a brother, Propan. His father was Miltan and his mother Kruton. And he had a son, Austin, who had been born in Japonia, the continent that lay west of Dolen Izmet over the sea.

"As a young man in the camp he was nervous," Dortan said. "Strong. Talented. He worked harder than any of the slaves, and even the guards respected him for it." He was quiet for a moment. "But he looked down on all the others. He thought they were weak. When a disturbance came and he saw a chance, he ran." He looked at his hands. "I really hope to find him. To see him. At least to know that my son is alive."

His voice was warm, but Senko had been listening carefully. When he came to the reason his family had come to Dolen Izmet, his words rang false, the way they did whenever someone lied in front of her. Without a sound she sent the warning to her companions' minds: He's lying about why they came.

Rank 5 turned around on the driver's bench. "Old man," he said, not unkindly, "we saved you. Tell us the truth."

Dortan sighed, and for a moment he looked even older.

"My wife and I were driven out," he said. "In Japonia they don't think much of my kind. Dragonborn are lower than slaves there. If an outsider is of no use to them, they don't accept him." He had grown up alone in a boarding house, and he had met his wife there. In the end a fisherman offered to take them across the water in his boat. "Austin was six," he said. "That's how we came here."

Nobody said anything for a while. The wheels creaked. Before they came near the tavern, the party told the old man clearly that he must say nothing to anyone about what had happened at Stonecrack. Dortan nodded.

The Blood Cleric

The party now thought of the Dris Tavern as enemy ground. They did not know whether Drisko himself had come back. They hid the cart in thick bushes two or three hundred feet from the building. Melmundo told Dortan to stay with Vihar until he came back for him, and then went in alone, with Heri in his arms.

The bartender knew him and greeted him in a friendly way. When Melmundo asked for a healer, the man first suggested a drink called cave grape, which he said cured most things. When Melmundo explained that it was for the animal, the bartender thought about it seriously. The only healer he knew was Asen. Asen had gone into Drisko's office with Austin about ten minutes before.

"Oh, I wouldn't go in there, if I were you," he said.

Melmundo bought him a beer and went in anyway.

The office was dim. Two men turned to the door. Austin, the dragonborn paladin, was tall and heavily built, with his scales catching the lamplight. Asen was a blue-skinned tiefling with a cleric's composure. Melmundo heard the end of what they had been saying. The matter was serious, Asen was telling Austin, and Austin might soon be gone if he did not pull himself together.

Austin was surprised to see Melmundo alone. He looked irritated but not afraid.

Melmundo did not waste words. He held Heri out to Asen and begged him to help her.

Asen took the little creature and turned her over in his long fingers. "Well," he said, "a very rare animal. Where did you get it?" Something in his voice suggested a price might be named.

"Not even for two hundred," Melmundo said at once. "I wouldn't sell her for a thousand. She's my best friend."

Asen smiled faintly and took out a dagger. He drew the blade across his own wrist. The blood did not drip. It moved, flowing through the air in a thin dark ribbon and sinking into Heri's belly. The potriffon shuddered. Then the blood came back out of her, as clean as it had gone in, and slid home into the vein it had come from. The cut closed.

Heri opened her eyes. She climbed up Melmundo's arm and settled on his shoulder, the way she always did.

The fee was a hundred and fifty gold. Melmundo counted it out and added twenty more as thanks. Asen made a small gesture, and a black opening appeared in the air beside him, a hole into somewhere else. He dropped the coins into it and it closed.

At the door he stopped and looked back at Austin.

"Be very careful," he said softly. "Because if I'm ordered to kill you, I won't hesitate. Buddy."

Austin did not move. "If you're so sure," he said calmly, "so be it."

Asen left.

Johnny

Eronar came in a little later. He paid the bartender a single gold coin for the cheapest beer in the house. When he asked, the bartender pointed him toward the office. Eronar went to the door, but he did not open it. He leaned close to the wood and listened.

A heavy hand fell on his shoulder.

"Hey, buddy, what are you eavesdropping on there, huh?"

The speaker was a fat dwarf with a huge beard, whom the whole tavern knew as Johnny, though his name was Walter. Nobody eavesdropped on that office, he told Eronar, not in this place. There were three people in there. And since the gentleman obviously had time on his hands, perhaps he would like to drink?

He put fifty gold on the bar. Eronar, who should have known better, matched it.

The first round went down. Before the second, Eronar reached under his coat for his alchemy jug. He had meant to make his drink a little less honest, but the jug was empty. He had poured every last drop of it into the guards and operators of Stonecrack on the night they fell. He drank the second round faster than the dwarf, set down the cup with a look of triumph, and fell flat on the floor.

Johnny raised his arms. The regulars cheered. It was one more victory for a champion who had never lost.

Melmundo found his friend like that when he came out of the office. He lifted him onto a table and slapped him. Then he slapped him again, hard enough to hurt. Eronar opened his eyes and saw four Melmundos looking down at him, all of them worried, and none of them standing still.

Melmundo asked the bartender for coffee. The coffee did nothing. The bartender sighed, reached under the bar and brought out a pouch of fine powder. He stirred some of it into a cup of water.

"Give him a little of this," he said. It was asaltin.

Melmundo held Eronar's jaw open and poured it in. The effect was immediate. Eronar sat up with clear eyes, completely sober, and slightly ashamed. The bartender would not take money for it. He explained that Eronar had challenged Johnny, and that was not something people did twice. Melmundo pressed a gold coin into his hand anyway.

Then Melmundo looked across the room at the dwarf. Johnny was still enjoying the applause.

Twenty-Five

"A hundred gold," Melmundo said, and put it on the bar.

Johnny jumped up onto a table. "I'm Johnny, undefeated at drinking!" he shouted to the whole room. "My boy, if you want to challenge me, show me the money!"

Melmundo showed it. Johnny matched it, and the bartender took two hundred gold and kept it behind the counter. The crowd closed in around them. Somewhere in the noise, Senko came in through the door, saw what was happening and stopped to watch.

The rounds came. The aasimar was enormous, and his body was built for war. Every cup went down without trouble at first. The dwarf drank as if the stuff were water, wiped his beard and called for more. At the fifth round Johnny tore off his shirt, and the tavern roared.

Eronar was sober now and watching closely. As the dwarf moved, he felt the faint signs of magic coming from him. Some items were hidden on his body, under the great curtain of his beard, and Eronar could not see what they were.

The sixth cup was too much. Melmundo lifted it, and his hand stopped obeying him. The cup fell, and so did he. He did not lose consciousness, but he lay on the floor, and the fight was over.

Johnny had won his twenty-fifth match without a single loss. The crowd lifted him onto their shoulders and carried him around the room, half-naked and shining. The bartender handed him the pot.

Senko caught the bartender's eye and pointed at the big body on the floor. The man sighed, jumped over the bar and poured asaltin into Melmundo's mouth. Melmundo sat up sober. A second dose, the bartender warned, would leave him with a hangover.

"I won't drink anymore," Melmundo said solemnly. "Ever."

The Left Hand

Austin was waiting for them. Eronar opened the Bag of Holding and emptied it in front of him: three hundred and twenty-five harpy feathers, grey and bronze and bloodstained, piled on the floor of the office. Austin looked them over and paid. First came a purse of one thousand six hundred and twenty-five gold. Then he set a second purse beside it, four hundred more. Two thousand and twenty-five in all, or five hundred and six for each of the four of them.

Then Eronar asked the question he had come to ask.

What was the real reason Austin had sent them to that camp? Because they had found someone there. One of his parents.

The room changed. Austin said that the person was someone from his past who had chosen to stay and rot in the camp. He had spent three years there himself, working day and night. And he had become what he was now: Drisko's left hand.

"I am the left hand," he said. "I am the man he sends when there is a certain problem with unpleasant people." Every bandit in that camp obeyed him.

"Austin," Melmundo said, "we don't see you as an enemy. We see you as, I'd say, a friend." He promised that they would never use what they knew against him.

Eronar went on. "However much power you have," he said quietly, "you are still a slave to someone else."

Lightning crackled across Austin's scales. The ink bottle on the desk was in his hand, and then it was not, only black ink running through his fingers and broken glass on the floor.

Melmundo asked the others to leave so that he could speak to Austin alone. They would not go. Melmundo turned and walked out of the tavern, angry, and the door banged behind him.

Eronar raised his hands. "I am not a man who seeks conflict," he said. "I am a scholar, an archivist, a librarian, call me what you like."

Senko stepped forward. Her voice was softer.

"I don't think like him," she said. "I see you before me now as a strong man who has obviously been through a lot." She asked him whether fate held something greater for him than being someone's hand.

Austin was silent for a long time. Then he spoke, and it was not what any of them expected.

As a child he had been mocked for his parents, for his race and for his god. He had lost count of the people he had killed for it. In the camp he had watched his mother give in and go to the guards, and he had watched what they did to her in front of him. He had watched his father pretend that nothing was happening. So he had left all of it behind him and risen by his own strength alone, until the highest bandit of a whole sector trusted him above everyone else.

He looked at Eronar.

"Because there is truth behind your words, you make me want to tear your head off here and now," he said. "But you are right."

One of them took off a ring, one of their most precious possessions and a sign of everything they had achieved, and held it out to him as proof that they were not lying. Austin looked at it. His tail moved and flicked the ring back into the hand that had offered it.

"We have shed blood together," he said. He would come.

Father and Son

Outside, behind the bushes, Rank 5 and Dortan were playing dice in the back of the cart and arguing cheerfully over a throw.

The old dragonborn looked up as the others came through the branches. He saw who was walking with them, and the dice fell from his hand.

"Austin!" he said. "You're alive!"

He climbed down without any of the morning's stiffness and reached up with trembling fingers to touch his son's face, as if it might be another illusion. "He told me," Dortan whispered. "He told me you would become something. Something great!"

Austin had broken an ink bottle with his bare hand less than an hour before. Now he broke down himself. He pulled his father into his arms, and a single tear ran down his scales.

When he could speak again, he turned to the party. "Thank you for doing what I wanted so much but could not."

"Don't mention it, bro," said Rank 5. "You know, Rank Five is always here."

Dortan told his son, in a rush, what had become of Stonecrack. It was the story they had agreed on: the slaves had killed all the guards, and then the spiders had come and killed the slaves. The camp was a home for spiders now. The party added that Tomon, the only operator of the camp, was dead.

Austin promised to bury the whole matter and keep it secret. He felt that he owed them for his father's life. But there was a problem, and its name was Asen.

Asen was annoying and arrogant, and Austin could not stand him, but he was loyal to Drisko and would give his life for him. He still had the blood of every one of them from the healing, and he could use it to find them, though only when he cast the spell, not at every moment. He had already sent people after them. Those scouts had followed the party toward Stonecrack for exactly two hours, as ordered, and had seen them enter the camp, talk with the operators and leave almost at once toward the hill. Then they had gone back to report.

"He's as smart as he is annoying," Austin said. "He'll put two and two together quickly." Stonecrack no longer made money for Drisko, and sooner or later Drisko would notice. Drisko respected Austin greatly and would take his word over anyone else's. But Asen's word was another matter.

"What I would propose," Austin said, "is that you eliminate Asen completely." He would help. In a duel between the two of them alone, he said, the odds were even.

Rank 5 asked what would happen to Austin afterward if they killed Asen with his help. Eronar suggested that Austin send them back toward the camp and then lure Asen after them. Rank 5 took the idea further: take Asen to Stonecrack itself, where the spiders and Austin would be on their side and nobody would ever learn that he had died there. A new delivery of slaves was due within a day or two. Someone even said the camp might be run again one day with paid workers. Whatever happened, Asen had to be dealt with first.

Austin took his father aside, and the two dragonborn talked quietly for a while, away from the others.

The Pact

When they met again, Austin had made his plan.

"I'll bring him out," he said. "Make him be alone. I know how to get on his nerves."

Melmundo asked Senko a question in Celestial, the language of his god, which the dragonborn did not speak. Austin knew three languages, but not that one. Is he lying?

No, Senko answered in the same language. He speaks only the truth.

Melmundo thought of the medallion of Hikari, which had saved him in the fight with Tumbo. He wanted to ask Austin about it, but he did not. It was Austin who raised it. He had seen what came out of the medallion that day, and he said he could not explain it.

They told him things in return. A spider man, a drider named Ivak, had helped them take Stonecrack, and all he had asked in return was the camp as his new lair. And there was a necromantic power at Pueven, the cave near the camp, which they meant to deal with.

Austin listened. About the slave cart, which would arrive in about a day, he gave them a warning. "I advise you not to meddle with that directly."

Then he made his request. The party had a contact in a village, the place Derek Fauner had gone. "Take my father and bring him there," Austin said. He would be safe there. After Asen was dead and the slave cart was stopped, Austin would do one more thing. "I will do something I believed I would never do," he said, and he would not say more. In about a week he would meet them at Ost Taker.

Dortan was told to stay with Vihar and the cart and to keep well away from the fight. The old man did not argue.

"My boy is alive," he said. "I must live for someone."

Eronar asked him to fix the cart's wheel while he waited, and Senko lent him her hammer. Dortan weighed it in his hand and made some remark about young people and their newfangled things, and for a moment everyone laughed.

The Horn

Austin walked into the Kartella camp alone, raised a horn to his lips and blew.

The guards came from everywhere, from the barracks and the yards and the roofs. Austin faced them with a look of cold fury.

"It turns out we have a traitor in the group," he said.

He handed them a poster with a man's face on it. They were to find him, he ordered, even if it took a week. The man on the poster had betrayed the organization four years ago, and Drisko had executed him with his own hands. Most of these guards were new and did not know that. They took the poster, saluted and went off into the countryside after a dead man.

The camp was empty.

The party moved into position near Asen's quarters. Senko and Eronar took cover behind a building. Rank 5 and Melmundo climbed onto a roof where the guards had stood a few minutes before. Austin gave them the signal: when he said Friend, they would attack. Eronar touched Melmundo's arm and murmured a spell that sharpened his strength. Melmundo reminded the others what they faced: Asen was a cleric, and he could heal.

Austin kicked the door open and called Asen out.

Friend

Asen came out into the empty yard and looked around. Where were the guards?

The two of them argued, the paladin and the cleric, with the bitterness of men who had disliked each other for years. Asen said he would send people wherever he liked, and told Austin to mind his own work.

Asen looked up at the rooftops.

On the roof, Melmundo shifted his weight. He was very large. The tile under his foot cracked, broke loose and slid off the edge, and it shattered on the ground right in front of Asen's feet.

Austin put his hand over his face.

"I suggest," he told Asen, "you do the work yourself." Then, louder: "Friend."

A crossbow bolt from the roof hit Asen in the shoulder. A bolt of fire from behind the building struck him in the face. Eronar charged his pistol with arcane power and fired again. Melmundo spread his wings, great and shining, and dropped from the roof toward the cleric.

"Come here to me!"

Asen struck his own armor with his mace, and healing energy ran over him and closed his burns. He raised a light crossbow and shot Melmundo point-blank. The bolt bounced off the aasimar's muscles. Then Asen bit into his own wrist. The blood that poured out did not fall. It twisted in the air into a weapon, a floating thing of red, and the weapon grew teeth. It bit into Melmundo's arm, and the wound burned with holy light.

Austin opened his jaws and breathed lightning into Asen's face.

Rank 5 slid down behind Melmundo's broad back, using him as a wall, and shot around him. The bolt went through Asen's other shoulder, and the cleric went down on one knee.

He got up again. He wiped the blood from his mouth and smiled.

"Fine, then," he said. "Let's play."

Senko stepped out and spread her hands, and fire poured over him. Asen's eyes glowed blood red. He threw a spell at Melmundo, and Melmundo shook it off. He snapped his fingers at Senko, and a heavy wave of sleep rolled over her mind. She held on, and stayed awake.

Austin answered with a spell of his own and stepped in close. He struck Asen twice, two heavy blows to the body, and Asen spat blood.

Asen saw how it would end. He turned and ran.

He did not get far. From the roof Rank 5 took aim one last time, and the bolt went straight through Asen's head. The blood cleric fell in the dust of the yard and did not move again.

Austin lifted the body as easily as a sack of grain and threw it away.

"You must leave immediately," he said.

They did not argue. They went back to where Dortan waited with the horse and the cart and Airiene. The old man's face lit up when he saw them all alive.

"Oh! You came back! Great!"

When they looked back, Austin had already gone.

The Road Nobody Uses

Everyone's wounds had healed. They turned the cart toward Selki.

Eronar unfolded the map and followed the directions that Derek Fauner had given them before he left. The route led onto a forest road that was uneven, but solid enough for a cart. Nobody seemed to have used it in a very long time. Three or four days of travel lay ahead. Senko had walked roads like this before, without ever caring where they went. Now she watched the trees closely.

"Be ready to fight all kinds of creatures," she told them. "These woods hold many dangerous monsters that we have not met."

Heri rode on Vihar's back, fully recovered and watchful. Melmundo spoke to her, and she flew ahead to scout and came back having seen nothing. But Senko's eyes were sharper. Far ahead, among the trunks, stood a cart loaded with wood and a few men around it.

They stopped out of sight. Rank 5 stayed with the cart to guard Dortan. Melmundo told the old man to stay where he was. Before they went on, Senko knelt by the side of the road. There were tracks in the soft ground, like a wolf's but far too big, heading west, toward the side of the road where their cart stood.

Melmundo sent Heri to find where the wolves were. Then he, Senko and Eronar walked on to the woodcutters.

Sky

One of the woodcutters was slapping another on the back of the neck when they arrived.

"He's an idiot!" he told the strangers, as if they had asked. "He stacked so much wood that the wheel broke, and now we have no tools to fix it."

Their guard stepped forward. He was calm and watchful, and his name was Sky. He asked whether the travellers had tools they could use. Then he looked past them at a tree where Eronar was trying to stay out of sight and politely invited him to come out. Eronar came out and gave his name.

Melmundo asked what they did. There were only three of them, Sky said, and they were one of the main groups sent out to gather wood.

"Don't misunderstand," he added. "I don't cut wood. I watch over the boys." These woods were dangerous. He knew these forests, he said, and as he said it his eyes went sharply to Senko.

Eronar said he knew tools a little. In about a quarter of an hour he had not only repaired the broken wheel but a second one as well, and the cart stood sturdier and moved more freely than before. One of the woodcutters watched Buzz4o, Eronar's fey bee, circling the group, and nervously asked whether it was dangerous.

"No," said Eronar. "She's a good little bee."

Sky said that he, his brother and all his kin were allergic to bees. He and his brother owned a tavern. Guarding was his main work, though at the moment he was on a break, and these woodcutters were his friends.

Senko listened to all of it, and she felt something strange. She could find no lie in anything he said, and no truth either. Her gift simply slid off him. This individual, she sent silently to Melmundo and Eronar, is very, very good at hiding the truth.

Sky seemed to see it in her face. He stopped and sighed.

"I've seen your kind," he said to her. "More than once. I just wasn't sure whether you could see the truth." He apologized, and Senko felt the wall around him come down. Now she could read him again.

In his world, he explained, there were people who played with truth and lies, and exposing them could get you killed. And the village was a secret.

"Certain people tried to find the location of the village," he said. "That cannot happen."

He noticed the badge of the Dojo that one of them wore and asked about their rank. Then he told them about the Wolves, the main police of the region. They were a special force above the ordinary guard and better trained. Their many ranks could be told by their badges, from wood at the bottom to diamond at the top. The Dojo, he said, was on good terms with the Wolves, and the village people would hardly care about it. Still, he had heard that certain people from the Dojo had been the ones trying to find the village.

"I'm an archivist," Eronar said. "But I have no interest in the village itself."

Sky accepted that. Before they parted, he invited them to his tavern. "It's called the Raven One," he said. "My brother works there." Eronar took out his notebook and wrote the name down.

The woodcutters went back into the trees to cut more wood. Heri returned, landed on Melmundo's shoulder and had seen nothing.

Teeth in the Dark

They found the wolves a little way down the road. Or rather, the wolves found them.

Senko saw them first, two huge grey shapes low in the undergrowth, gathering themselves to spring, and she shouted a warning just before they came.

Rank 5 did not waste a moment. He grabbed Dortan by the scruff of the neck and threw him off the cart, clear of the danger, then drew both daggers and stood ready over him. Senko threw sleep at the nearest wolf, but the beast was far too big and strong, and it kept coming.

Melmundo roared. The rage took him, and he bellowed at both wolves to come to him and swung his sword wildly at one. It bit him in return, closed its jaws on him and dragged him to the ground. The second wolf leapt at the cart and sank its teeth into Eronar's shoulder. He held on to the side of the cart and was not pulled down. He wrapped his dagger in arcane force and drove it into the wolf, and lightning cracked through the animal's fur.

Senko jumped onto a fallen log and called up her fox fire. Blue flame burst out of her in a ring fifteen feet wide. It burned the wolf beside her and threw it back, and the animal slammed into the side of the cart. The cart tipped and fell over, and Dortan, who had only just got up, ended up on the ground again.

Melmundo got back to his feet in a frenzy. His blade came down once more, and the first wolf's head fell away from its body.

The second wolf bit him. Eronar sent Buzz4o flying to the big man's side and fired a bolt of flame into the living wolf. It was hurt now, snarling and bleeding.

Rank 5 cartwheeled across the road and landed on the wolf's back. He drove both daggers into its neck and pulled up hard. The wolf shuddered under him and fell still.

Selki

The forest was quiet again.

They took what the wolves had to give: eight strong teeth, twelve claws and two huge pelts. The pelts could be made into clothes or tents, and the teeth and claws into daggers, arrows and darts. All of it went into the Bag of Holding, which Eronar kept. Then they pushed the overturned cart back onto its wheels, put Dortan back into it and moved on before anything else in those woods came looking for them.

The cart had not been properly repaired after its fall, and it rocked and creaked with every rut. Rank 5 drove, whistling badly. He let Vihar wander from one side of the road to the other until the whole cart leaned and nearly tipped over a second time. Dortan held on with both hands.

At last the trees thinned. The road came out of the forest onto an open plain under a wide sky, and Vihar went on across the grass when he was told to go on.

Ahead of them, in the middle of the plain, lay the village of Selki.
