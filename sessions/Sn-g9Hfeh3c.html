Chapter 5: The Village of Kyutuk

A Village That Waved

They had left a lot behind them on the road. Stonecrack belonged to the spiders now. Dortan had held his son Austin again after two years apart. Asen lay dead in the grass where the ambush had caught him, with Rank 5's arrow through his skull. Austin had asked them to take his father on to the village and said he would wait a few days. Then there had been the half-elf Sky and his cart, and two dire wolves whose teeth, claws and hides were now packed in the party's bags. Ahead, the old dwarf Derek Fauner and his friend Dr. Philip Flane were supposed to be waiting.

The village came into view late in the afternoon. It was small and tucked away, the kind of place that did not appear on Imperial maps. The Compass Gang drove in expecting suspicion and found the opposite. People in the fields straightened up and waved. A woman at a well raised her bucket in greeting. It looked as though visitors were rare here and welcome when they came.

Sky's cart was parked near the road.

Eronar saw it first and slowed. Senko came up beside him without a sound, and the two of them looked at the cart and then at each other. They had left Sky's group behind on the road. No cart could have passed them, and no cart could have reached Selki first. Yet the half-elf was leaning against it as if he had been there for hours.

"Welcome," Sky said, smiling.

They asked him about the place. It held about forty people, he told them, maybe forty-six now. A few newcomers had arrived on horseback from a camp in the west, and Sky's men had tested them to see whether they meant trouble. They had come to live, nothing more. Anyone new should introduce themselves to the mayor, Roartek, who could usually be found in the meeting hall. The mayor liked to know who came into his village.

"And how did you get here before us?" one of them asked.

Sky's smile widened. "We have our secrets, don't we?"

Senko watched him with her fox's stillness. She could tell when a man was hiding something behind a pleasant face, and Sky was hiding a great deal. Rank 5 had stopped looking at Sky altogether. He was looking at the ground around the cart, where black feathers lay in the dust and caught on the wheel spokes. They were large ones, much too large for a crow.

He said nothing yet. He filed it away, the way rogues do.

Children ran shrieking between the houses chasing a ball. Melmundo stood tall above the whole scene and watched them with an odd softness on his broad face. He had been small once too, a long time ago and far from here, and the game made him think of it.

Pil's Stables

The stables were a big barn surrounded by meadows, with carts parked in rows beside it. The keeper came out wiping his hands. He was a ratfolk, small and quick, with whiskers that twitched as he looked them over. His name was Pil.

He went straight to Vihar, as a good horseman would, and his face fell.

He lifted a hoof and clicked his tongue. It had never been cleaned, not properly, not in years. He pulled back the horse's lip and found the teeth crooked, the kind of crooked that made every mouthful of hay a struggle. Vihar stood patiently through it all. He had been patient for a long time.

"This horse has been neglected," Pil said. He added, carefully, that he also sold horses if they wanted a replacement.

"No," Melmundo said at once. "Heal him."

Pil looked up at him, and then at the rest of them: the horned tiefling, the blue Vedalken, the little fox-woman. His eyes stayed a moment on Senko.

"Kitsune," he said. "They'd call you Minx where I come from. Same as me. Same as the tabaxi."

The village was almost five years old, he told them, and he had come here to get away from what was said about folk like him where he used to live. "People see the ears, they see the tail, they see my face," he said, "and mostly they don't like little folk."

"People will grow wiser," Melmundo told him. "Give it time."

The party introduced itself as the Compass, more tourists than fighters. Melmundo's sword hung at his side the whole time, and Pil's eyes kept going back to it.

"Don't worry," the barbarian said. "It's just a walking cane."

Pil asked about payment. An examination was about two gold, he said, and serious treatment could be ten or twenty. Melmundo put twenty gold in his hand before he had finished, and the ratfolk stared at the coins as if nobody had ever paid him in advance. He started work immediately. He would need an hour or two with the horse.

While he worked he talked a little more. He had been a groom once. "I ran away from the capital," he said, and would not say why. When someone mentioned the doctor, Dr. Phil, he shook his head firmly. He had nothing to do with him.

Dortan had not come inside. The old blue dragonborn stood by the stable door with his hands clasped, watching people walk past. He had spent five years seeing the same faces behind the same fences, and now there were too many faces and too much open sky at once. When the party came out Melmundo knelt without a word, lifted the old man onto his shoulders, and carried him the rest of the way like a child at a fair.

Dots on Their Backs

It was twenty-five to six when they walked through the village toward the tavern. The light had turned golden and long.

Melmundo noticed something about the children as they passed. Some of their shirts had one dot sewn or painted on the back between the shoulder blades, and some had two. It might have been a game or a fashion. But Melmundo was an aasimar, and he knew where wings would sit on a back if a child had them. He looked at the dots for a long time and kept his thoughts to himself.

The tavern sign showed a black raven's wing. Rank 5 could read, which surprised some people, and he read the name out: the Raven One. Then he thought of the black feathers around Sky's cart, and a small cold idea settled in his mind.

The Raven One

Nobody guarded the door. When the Compass Gang came in, the patrons raised their glasses to the strangers as if they had been expected.

A barmaid came over, and Melmundo did the introductions, as he usually did. Senko, he added, did not talk much.

"Well then, nice to meet you," the barmaid said. "I'm Miranda, by the way."

She told them there were only two places to sleep in the village: here at the Raven One, or Rando's motel. The rooms here were wider. Then she listed the menu: soup with meatballs, tripe soup, alphabet soup, bread, cheese and meat. A bottle cost ten gold. A beer cost four copper.

Eronar did a quick sum and stood up.

"A beer for everyone!" he said. "And four schnapps for our table."

The room roared. Mugs banged on the wood, someone slapped his shoulder, and for a moment the blue archivist from the Archive was the most popular man in Selki.

Dortan drank slowly and talked. Dragonborn were not dragons, he explained, in case anyone wondered. They were half-human and half-dragon, and they did not turn into dragons, whatever the stories said. He was about a hundred and eighty years old. His people lived to two hundred, sometimes more. He was a blue chromatic, he said, with a tail. He could breathe lightning.

"If you see a chromatic dragonborn with a tail," he said, "most of them are big racists." He smiled and turned his cup in his old hands. "But I love everyone. I was a slave for five years. How could I be a racist?"

Feathers and Fur

Two patrons came over from a nearby table. One was an aarakocra, tall and feathered, with a hard, bright stare. The other was a fennec, small, with huge ears and arms that looked a little too long for the rest of him.

The fennec's eyes went to the badge on Eronar's chest.

"You're from the Archive?"

"Eronar," he said, which was answer enough.

"Eronar. Interesting name. Nice to meet you," said the aarakocra. "I'm Maktel."

Something in how she leaned in made the table tense. She seemed to like the idea of testing herself against a member of the Archive a little too much. The fennec, Creed, told her not to scare the boy, wished the party a pleasant dinner, and drew her away.

Across the room Sky was shaking a man's hand.

The man was big and loud, and everything about him was meant to be seen. He held the half-elf's hand a moment too long. "I wish you a good trip too, sonny," he said. "And know that we're waiting for you. You and your ugly brother."

When he turned, the whole room could see the large emblem on his back. Eronar recognised it at once, and the recognition was not pleasant.

"I'm a damn historian," he muttered when the others looked at him. "I work in the archives. I'm an archivist." Then, more quietly: "That's Ursa."

They knew what that meant. Ursa was a crew like Drisko's, only bigger and higher up. The man with the emblem was Maverick, its leader.

The Man Who Owed a Favour

When Maverick had gone back to his own table, Sky came over and sat down heavily beside Melmundo. A second man came with him. He was taller and quieter, and a scar ran from his throat up to his chin, an old wound from a fight that had taken his voice. This was Rex, Sky's brother. The two of them owned the Raven One together.

"The favour I owe is being called in now," Sky said. "They're forcing me to fight in the PKB."

Melmundo did not know the word, so Sky explained. The PKB was the Underground Colosseum of Combat. Fighters came there from other empires and even other continents. There were four champions, and anyone who beat one of them became very rich. The people who bet there were influential and dangerous, and a single fight could move one or two million gold.

Sky and his brother knew Maverick well. Long ago Maverick had saved Sky's life, and debts like that never quite got paid off.

"I don't consider myself a good fighter," Sky said, "but that man has decided to make my life hard even here."

Melmundo frowned. "I wouldn't let people treat me the way they treat you."

Rex rested a dagger on the barbarian's shoulder. It was not a threat, only a mute man's way of putting a hand there, and Melmundo understood it. He offered his help anyway, whatever it turned out to be.

Sky said they would talk again tomorrow or the day after, and that the party might be able to help. Then he added a warning, half amused and half embarrassed. They should not sleep at the Raven One. He had a sister who liked to put her hands where they didn't belong. Rando's motel would be safer.

Then he took a white mask from his coat and put it on.

He walked to the open window. Rex stepped in front of him, broad enough to hide him from the room. Behind Rex's back something happened that most of the room did not see: a shape folded smaller and darker. Wings beat once against the frame, and a bird went out into the evening.

Maktel was at the window in a moment, feathers raised, staring after it. Then she turned and left the tavern without a word.

Rank 5 had seen all of it.

Splinter Mishok

All evening someone had been watching their table. He had a knack for being in the corner of your eye and gone when you turned. Rank 5 had noticed him early. Now he looked straight into the shadows by the wall and called out a name to see what it would bring out.

"Mishok."

A ratfolk came forward, grinning, as if the name pleased him. He had a long coat and quick hands.

"I'm Splinter Mishok," he said. He worked as a fence and dealt in odd goods and tools. He had come over because the party looked like people with money. "If you need to buy or sell something under the table," he said, "I'm your man."

He opened his coat like a book. There was a dagger, ammunition, and a vial of poison. Under the table there was more. He had firearms, a new technology that few people used yet. He had a shortsword, a keen dagger, and a whip. There was poison at a hundred gold a dose and something he called Assassin's Blood, a small mirror, lock picks, files, smoke grenades, and a coil of silk rope.

Rank 5 glanced at the lock picks and shrugged. He had his own thieves' tools.

Melmundo bought the silk rope for twelve gold.

Eronar leaned in. Did Mishok ever come across magic items? Anything to do with curses, an artifact or a catalyst?

Mishok's whiskers twitched. He could find such things, he said. But it would take time.

Between Bear and Fox

Later Eronar found himself sitting between Maverick and Creed. Perhaps he had chosen to, or perhaps they had made room for him on purpose. The Ursa leader was in a talkative mood. He was in Selki on business, he said. His crew had been at Tom and Jerry recently and at the night market, meeting acquaintances. Rank 5 heard the name of his own organisation and kept his face still.

Maverick nodded toward the window Sky had flown from. "We do business with him," he said. "The elf. The learning is very useful."

Eronar thought the whole arrangement was strange, and Sky stranger still.

Senko, across the table, was not watching Maverick. She was watching Creed's long arm, which was sliding slowly behind Eronar's back toward his coat and his pockets. She did not move or speak aloud. She sent her words straight into Eronar's mind: Watch your left.

Eronar turned his head. Creed's hand was already back behind his own body, scratching idly at his side.

"You have long arms for such a small body," Eronar said.

The fennec bared his teeth in something like a smile. "I don't like blue people."

Eronar pressed on. He mentioned Drisko, and Maverick laughed out loud that anyone would say the name so calmly. Yes, he knew Drisko. Then Eronar asked about someone called Trisk and wrapped the question in a story. He was not a good liar, and the two of them looked at each other and did not quite believe him.

Soon after, Maverick stood and wished Eronar well until they met again. Creed sprang over Eronar's head and landed on the big man's shoulder, and the two of them went out into the dark.

When they were gone, Rank 5 leaned across the table and told the others what he had seen. The mask, the window, the wings. Sky could turn into a bird. Sky could fly. That explained how his cart had beaten them here. It also raised other questions.

Miranda came by to clear the plates. When she heard they were thinking of the motel, she sniffed. "I'll say it's more expensive there than at my place."

They thanked her anyway. Sky had warned them about his sister.

The Statue and the Mayor

At five to seven the village was half dark, and lamps were being lit in the windows. Sky had said the mayor should know who came to his village, so they went to find him.

On the way they passed a statue of a figure called Kevdar. Several inscriptions were carved into its base, and they stopped to read them in the last of the light. One mentioned a blacksmith, Blacksmith Junior, and they did not know what to make of it.

The mayor's house was lit by candles. When they knocked, the unlocked door swung open by itself, and they heard a thump and a curse from inside. An old man was kicking a chest whose lid had stuck. When kicking did not work, he hit it with his cane.

He was about eighty years old, broad in the shoulders and stiff in the knees.

"I'm the mayor, yes," he said. "I'm Mayor Roartek. Nice to meet you."

Melmundo introduced them. "I am Melmundo. The horned one next to me is Rank 5, the blue guy is Eronar, and the little fox is Senko."

A tiny old woman appeared at the mayor's elbow, a halfling far older than he was, and smacked him with her own cane for no reason anyone could see.

"I am Grandma Urali," she said. "Nice to meet you."

She was his mother. When her son asked her, she shuffled off and left them to it.

Roartek already knew who they were. Derek Fauner had told him the party had saved him. He was a retired private of the Wolves, the region's police, and had left forty years ago.

"The young ones became corrupt," he said, and his jaw worked. "They look the other way. They no longer fight for the empire."

The party told him about their horse, which had felt unwell and was now resting with Pil. "Good lad, Pil," the mayor said. "He'll take care of it." Derek, he added, was staying at Randall's motel.

Then he told them what was keeping him awake. Over the last few days big holes had opened in most of the farms, and tunnels were collapsing under the fields. Some people said they had seen huge creatures with tentacles. His mother was a bit of a druid. She had asked the earth what was happening, and nature had answered: ankhegs.

"I sent some guards to look," Roartek said. "Two days ago. They haven't come back."

He asked them for help. Melmundo offered it at once and arranged to meet him the next day. Roartek suggested around one or two o'clock.

Before they left, Eronar asked whether the mayor had ever heard of something called the Corpus curse. The old man shook his head. He had not.

Randall's Motel

As they reached the motel, an old guest was leaving, and he threw his key straight into the keeper's face on his way out.

"Annoying ingrates!" Randall shouted after him, and then turned to the newcomers. "Welcome!"

They took four rooms and he handed over the keys. In the yard was a target game, two gold for thirty minutes. Melmundo paid and shot for an hour by lantern light, laughing at his misses and cheering his hits. One arrow went wild and passed so close to Eronar's eye that the Vedalken felt the wind of the fletching.

Eronar barely noticed, because across the common room Derek Fauner was sitting with Dr. Philip Flane.

The Ruins of Alduin

Derek was very old, a dwarf whose name every archaeologist on the continent knew. Flane was younger, a doctor with an intent face. He wore a red medallion at his throat, decorated with golden waves. They made room for Eronar, and soon the others gathered too.

Flane opened a book to a map. He was planning an expedition, he said, to the ruins of Alduin.

The Yanti had lived there once. Theirs had been one of the first civilisations and one of the most advanced, a monarchy ruled by a pharaoh and his advisers. Then some cataclysm had wiped them out. Nobody had ever crossed the waters around Alduin. Mighty sea creatures and whirlpools guarded them, as if the land itself drove everyone away.

He touched the medallion. It could tame the sea beasts, he said. He had tested it on one, and it had not attacked him. He had dug it up about two years ago on an island near Japonia, the continent to the west.

"I am an archaeologist, boy," he said, when Eronar looked at it with open hunger. "I have artifacts like this."

He had hired the master brothers of Selki to build a strong boat. It would take about ten days.

The talk turned to the ankhegs. Flane had heard the stories too: huge beasts that came up out of holes, ate the crops and tore up the fields. "I would go and look," he said, "but I'm not stupid."

Then Eronar asked the question he had been carrying for weeks. Did they know a dragon called Samuil Jack?

They did not.

Did they know a man called Umbro Hebi?

Both scholars froze and looked at each other.

Flane spoke first, slowly. He had not heard that name in many years. Umbro Hebi was a scientist who experimented with mutations.

Eronar explained. Samuil Jack had possessed Dortan and spoken through him, and had told Eronar to learn about Umbro Hebi if he wanted to understand the Corpus curse.

"I am already affected by it," he said. "I'm marked."

Derek put his dagger on the table and asked how. For a moment nobody breathed. Then the old dwarf put the dagger away.

The Corpus Curse

"Umbro Hebi is a dangerous man," Derek said. "I know him personally."

Many years ago, he told them, Hebi had perfected a magic that made a person mutate into a beast, into their most primal form. The curse stayed on whoever was marked until Hebi himself removed it. It gave benefits, but it also had huge drawbacks.

Eronar's voice was steady, but only just. "This man apparently ran experiments in my home town. And my mother was actually a victim of one of these curses."

Derek looked at him for a long moment. Hebi had a reputation, he said. His name had once been in the bingo book, the list of the wanted, and later someone had taken it out.

How did the marking work, Eronar asked.

There was a sharp pain where you were marked, Derek said, usually on the neck or the back or somewhere else that could be seen. Most magic could not lift it. Even Remove Curse failed.

"The effect," the old dwarf said, "seems to come from Shadow magic. It would be very bad if he ever found a person who wields Shadow magic."

Senko sat very still. The shadows in the corner of the room seemed to lean toward her the way they always did. She asked one question in her quiet voice: did Umbro Hebi have any interest in Kaguya?

Derek's answer did not bring any comfort. Hebi was very evil, he said, and twisted. Whatever he wanted, it would not be good.

Ten Days

Derek gathered himself and brought them back to practical matters. The expedition was a paid one, and he wanted them on it. They would leave in ten days. They had to cross the river, and the trip by boat would take about another ten.

"I won't lie to you," he said. "It's dangerous. You may die." Devastating winds blew over Alduin and stopped anyone from flying there. People who had tried reported creatures made of air that drove them back: elementals. "No one has set foot in those ruins for centuries," Derek said, "and for a reason."

They agreed anyway. The old dwarf banged his fist on the table, and the cups jumped. Then the Compass Gang was with them, for now.

The door opened and Dortan came in.

Derek stared, then stood up so fast his chair scraped. "So you stayed alive!"

They had been slaves together, and the old dragonborn's eyes filled. "They treated me very well," he said, nodding at the party. "Thanks to them I even got to see my son."

It was late. Flane nodded to them all and went home. Before he left with Dortan, Derek drew Eronar aside.

"What you're doing matters," he said quietly. "For me, for the doctor, for a lot of scholars."

Eronar admitted that he had not really left the Archive. He had come alone because he did not have access to the information he needed. The old dwarf understood. They agreed that the Archive did not need to hear about this.

Then Derek and Dortan went out together into the night, two old men who had survived the same fences and had a lot to say to each other. The party went upstairs to sleep.

A Rough Morning

The morning of the twenty-sixth of April, a Tuesday, came grey and early, and Senko woke first, as she always did.

She dressed, slipped down the corridor to Eronar's door, and cast a small illusion into his room. Eronar opened his eyes to find Creed leaning over his bed, smiling with all his teeth.

"Good morning," said the fennec.

Eronar lashed out, and his hands passed straight through the image. Down the corridor someone was trying not to laugh.

Melmundo's morning went worse. Heritobibubka, his potriffon, had slept in Senko's room, and he went to fetch her. He knocked on the door, and when it opened Heri launched herself at his face, all claws and beak, pecking. Melmundo slapped at her several times. In the middle of the tangle Buzz4o joined in. Eronar's buzzarect may have mistaken the noise for an attack or just did not like being woken. It drove its sting into the barbarian.

The pain was huge. Melmundo staggered, gray-faced and badly hurt, and Eronar hurried to help him.

Bitaka

The village flea market was called Bitaka. From outside it was an ordinary house, but inside it was as big as a hangar, crowded with stalls and with people coming and going. It was about five years old, like the village, and took anywhere from ten to three hundred gold a day.

Eronar went looking for a gem. "It is something that will be the heart of my new servant," he told the seller, "so I want it to be good." He needed one worth about a hundred gold.

The seller had small, medium and large stones, several rubies and a sapphire, but nothing at that price. He offered the sapphire for twenty gold. High-tier gems, he explained, went into magic tattoos, magic items and crafting. Low-tier ones were mostly for jewellery.

"There's a goblin in town called Ardeo," he said. "He has a similar trade to this one." Ardeo might have what Eronar wanted.

Eronar's eye fell on an empty glass vial the seller had been using as a vase. He bought it for five silver.

Melmundo, still pale and moving stiffly, went to a potion stall run by a bearded gnome with surprisingly big arms. Did he have anything for wounds?

The gnome had his special Minor Healing Potions, but only two. Twenty-five gold each, forty-five for both. "A normal potion costs fifty gold," he added, to show what a bargain it was. Senko came up behind him silently, and the gnome nearly jumped out of his beard. Melmundo paid for both potions.

Rank 5 had wanted one too, but they cost more than he had expected. He bought rations instead for ten gold. They were cheaper here than anywhere else he had seen.

Then Buzz4o, perhaps out of guilt, landed squarely on Melmundo's face and stuck there. Warmth spread from it, and the barbarian's wounds closed until he was almost whole again. Healing tired the creature, and it would need rest afterward. Melmundo peeled it off, looked it in its many eyes, and gave it the middle finger.

Vihar's Keeper

In the morning light Selki looked busy and content. Villagers were out working the farms, and stalls along the lane were selling bacon. Pil was waiting at the stables, and his face was serious.

He had examined Vihar properly now. The horse's legs were failing after years of bad treatment. It was an illness like arthritis, and the horse would be slower now, until he healed. It could be cured in one or two months, maybe with an operation.

Melmundo paid for the treatment. Then he asked for something more.

"I want you to promise me you will take good care of Vihar," he said. If the party did not come back from where they were going, he wanted Pil to look after the horse for the rest of his life.

Pil promised. Normally, he said, that kind of care would cost a hundred and twenty gold.

"I am willing to do this for free."

Melmundo promised him a hundred gold if they did come back.

Then Pil showed them his horses. His pride was a warhorse bred from brown to black, with a mutation for speed. It was faster than anything the party had seen and strong enough to carry three riders. It cost four hundred and fifty gold. There was a second big black warhorse with a mutation for endurance, a little slower but very hard to bring down. For smaller purses he had a white pony with a speed mutation for thirty-five gold and an ordinary white riding horse for seventy-five.

While the others looked at horses, Senko drifted around the barn as she usually did, looking at everything. In a corner she found a coin purse holding six hundred and twenty gold. It seemed to be most of what Pil owned. She weighed it in her small hand and said nothing about it.

"I'm not from the neighbourhood," Rank 5 said when they asked his opinion. "I don't care about horses. I'm Rank 5."

Melmundo was already counting in his head. In the Bag of Holding they had the dire wolf pelts and teeth, and the plate armour taken from Tomon. All of it was worth perhaps five hundred gold if they could sell it. "Won't we need a horse for future expeditions anyway?" he asked.

They said they would think it over. First they would look for the goblin Ardeo.

Screams from the North

They had barely left the stable yard when the screaming started.

It came from the north end of the village and got louder as it came. Villagers ran past them with pitchforks and picks in their hands. Some were running toward the noise and some were running away from it. The ground shuddered under the party's feet.

From the north came the sound of earth breaking open, and something huge was climbing out.

The ankhegs had come to Selki, and there were many of them.
