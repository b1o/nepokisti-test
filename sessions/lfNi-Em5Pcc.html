Chapter 3: The Attack on the Camp

The Cave Above the Nest

The harpy nest was finished. Broken sticks and bloody feathers lay all over the top of the hill, and the wind carried the smell of the birds out over the valley. Airiene had gone. She had taken the two baby harpies, said her goodbyes and promised that they would meet again. Somewhere below, out of sight beyond the trees, stood Stonecrack.

Eronar took out his sundial and turned it to the low sun. "About half past five," he said.

The tavern was six or seven hours away. The four of them looked at the sky, and then at the dark band of forest between them and the road. Nobody wanted to be on that road after dark, so they dropped the idea of going back. They sat down among the rocks to rest for an hour, and none of them said much.

Melmundo was the first to notice something. He was sitting very still, his big shoulders loose, and he heard air moving where there should have been only stone. A moment later Eronar found where it came from: a dark gap in the side of the hill, half hidden behind fallen rock.

"There," the Vedalken said, and pointed.

Rank 5 crouched at the opening and let his tiefling eyes take in the dark. Senko came up behind him. Her darkvision showed her what he had missed: the rubble at the mouth was fresh. Something had brought down a small landslide not long ago and tried to block the cave.

Eronar picked up a stone, spoke a word over it and handed it to Melmundo, and it glowed with a steady white light. Senko flicked a bead of fire into the dark. It flew a long way before it died. The tunnel ran back two hundred feet or more.

Melmundo went in first. He had to bend nearly double. In the light of the stone the walls were covered in long scratches, three and four at a time. They were claw marks, and the party knew those claws. The harpies had been here and had attacked something.

Something else had been here as well. A thick white rope of web shot out of the dark and hit Eronar in the chest. Before he could shout, it had bound his arms. A giant spider came down the wall, legs moving one after another, lifted him onto its back as if he weighed nothing and ran off with him into the tunnel.

A voice spoke from deeper in the cave. The words were harsh and clicking: Undercommon. The spider stopped. It dropped Eronar on the stone.

Then the speaker came into the light.

From the waist up he looked like a drow, with dark skin, white hair and red eyes. Below the waist he was a spider, with a huge black body and eight legs, each one ending in a point like a spear. He stepped over Eronar and laid the point of one leg against his throat.

"Why are you here?" he asked.

Ivak

He wanted to know about the camp first. Did they work for the men down there? Were they with the slavers? The party told him the truth: they had been sent to deal with the harpies, and they had no love for the people at Stonecrack. The drider looked at them for a long moment. Then he drew a blade along the web that held Eronar and cut him free.

"I am what you simple mortals call a drider," he said.

Of the four of them, only Rank 5 had heard the word. Driders were the children of Lolth, he told the others quietly. The spider goddess of the dark elves looked much like the creature in front of them, and driders led the drow clans. But there was one thing he could not make fit, and he asked it straight out.

"Driders are female. Why are you male?"

"I don't care what they think," Ivak said. "I am what I am."

His clan had not thought the same. They had exiled him for it and wanted him dead, and he had hidden in the caves ever since. His name was Ivak. He had made a family here. He fathered children with the female giant spiders, and he led the party a little deeper and showed them his eggs, pale sacs fixed to the stone. One egg held two hundred to four hundred young, he said. Most of them died. Seven in ten never grew.

The rest had been dying too. The harpies hunted his children whenever they went out to feed, killing some and carrying others off. The camp was worse. Workers from Stonecrack had come up into his cave to harvest spider silk.

"They took the lives of over fifty of my children," Ivak said. They had torn his lair apart and wrecked everything that lived in it.

Melmundo stepped forward and told the spiders around them, in his plain way, that he was their friend. Then he reached into his pack and tossed Ivak a harpy head, a trophy from the fight on the hill. Ivak caught it, sniffed it and threw it up into the dark. A spider caught it in its web.

After that they could talk.

The party had had a plan of their own for the camp, and it had failed when one of their group flew away. They still wanted the slaves free. They could not do it alone. They had counted about fifteen armed guards. Senko spoke to the others silently with her Message spell: she had seen a hole at the back of the camp, she told them, and stairs at one of the towers.

Ivak listened. He feared one thing, and that was fire. The camp had fire arrows. But four strangers and his children together were another matter.

"There is strength in numbers," he said.

He had one condition. "The iron mine below stays with me and my children." He did not care about the slaves. Only the armed men were his enemies. The party agreed. They would go back down, win the guards' trust and keep them busy. At about two in the morning Ivak and six giant spiders would come over the walls.

The Archive Symbol

There had to be a way to signal him. Eronar sat down on a flat stone and took two matching stones from his kit. He worked on them for a quarter of an hour, laying an infusion into each.

"A wizard?" Ivak asked.

"No, not a wizard. An artificer."

He gave one stone to Ivak and spoke into the other. Everyone in the cave heard his voice come out of the stone in the drider's hand. The test stones went dead after that. A real pair, Eronar explained, could be used once each way, and with one the party could reach Ivak when the time came.

While Eronar was bent over his work, Ivak's eyes had settled on the symbol he wore. It was the mark of the Archive.

"I don't blame you for it," Ivak said. "I just know which group you are from."

Many years ago, he said, he and his closest companion had lived in a cave called Pueven. A woman named Charlie von Zeo had come into it, blind with rage, and challenged them. He never understood why. He had barely escaped. That was what the symbol meant to him. And yet one of his oldest friends was a drow named Elar, who belonged to the Archive too.

"A past I took no part in," Eronar said, "but yes, I understand." He wrote the name Elar in his notes.

For him the Archive was not the point now. There was a dwarf in Stonecrack, he told Ivak, and he meant to find him. He gave the drider his name. Ivak said he would remember it, and promised that none of his children would hurt the party.

When the others were getting ready to leave, Eronar took a wrong turn in the dark and lost his way. The passage dropped into a pit full of bones and whole skeletons, the place where Ivak fed. Between two stones at the bottom something glowed: a golden sword, shining in the dark. Beside it stood a chest.

He found his way back and asked the drider whether he used that sword.

"We'll talk about extras when the job is done," Ivak said.

Back to Stonecrack

Night had fallen when they came out onto the hill. Melmundo called into the dark for the horse, and Vihar came with the cart rattling behind him. Its wooden wheels were badly broken, but the cart still rolled. They ate a ration each, patched the wheels as well as they could and started down. It took two hours to reach Stonecrack.

The gates opened for them. Tomon came out to meet them and asked whether the harpy problem was solved. He saw the feathers heaped in the cart.

"So I'll take it that you're telling the truth," he said.

One harpy had come at the camp from their direction, he added, and he pointed. A lone harpy hung by its neck from a beam, and bandits were plucking its feathers. Then he grew friendly. There was food and drink in the cellar. There would be a celebration, but first the slaves had to work hard today, and if the guests wanted to help with the digging, they were welcome. He whistled, and a bandit came running.

Eronar said he would take a look at the lift.

A Day of Work

The slave quarters were a long low room that smelled of sweat and damp straw. Most of the people who slept there spent their whole lives in it, eating and sleeping and going out only to work. They were fed once a day. Picks and shovels hung along the wall, and Melmundo and Rank 5 each took one down.

Tumbo was waiting for them. He was Tomon's brother and bigger than anyone else in the camp, and he led them to a passage about ten feet down that had caved in. He wanted it open, wide enough for a cart. Melmundo swung his pick as if the rock had wronged him. Rank 5 dug as much as the Aasimar did, and more carefully. In an hour they had a passage twenty or thirty feet long that a cart could pass through.

At the lift a guard helped Eronar. The mechanism was jammed and the chain had snapped, and the guards had had nothing to fix it with. Eronar worked for an hour and a half. When he was done the chain held, and the mechanism was stronger than it had been when it was new.

Before they split up, Eronar had asked Senko for one thing: to find out from the slaves what she could about Austin, about Derek, and about Tomon.

She found an old dragonborn kneeling beside a horse and washing it with two white rags. Senko did not go near him. She sent her voice straight into his head.

He started. A bandit shouted at him for slacking. With her next message Senko gave him a way to answer: one nod for yes, two for no. The old man asked the bandit for a short rest and was given ten minutes.

Then Senko asked whether he knew a dragonborn named Austin.

The old man scrambled to his feet and rushed toward her. The bandit caught him first. He kicked the slave down and beat him until he bled. Senko stepped in and stopped it, lifted the dragonborn and carried him aside. He weighed almost nothing. Years of bad food had left him skin and bone.

"Austin is my son," he said. His voice was rough and broken. "He's my son. I haven't seen him in ages."

Senko told him that Austin was alive.

"So you say he's alive. That's good. My boy is alive."

His name was Dortan. He had been at Stonecrack five or six years. He, Austin and Austin's mother had been taken together and put to work. Austin had never been one to make friends, even as a child.

"He escaped two years ago," Dortan said. "He took advantage of there being no guard." Somehow the boy had opened his shackles and walked out through the main gate while nobody was watching. He did not look back.

Senko asked about Derek.

"Oh, Derek, that old lad." Dortan almost smiled. The dwarf had come a few months ago. He was kind and cheerful, never made trouble, and sometimes he fed Dortan from his own share.

Dortan told her his name, and asked for hers.

"Nice to meet you," Senko said. "For now it's better that you don't know my name."

Then she asked about Austin's mother, and his face changed.

"His mother is no longer here. She died a year ago in the mine." He hoped his son would come to see him one day.

He knew nothing about secret doors or weak points in the walls. About Tomon he said something strange: the camp leader was kinder than the rest. He hit Dortan more lightly than the others did and gave him scraps. Dortan thought Tomon knew whose father he was. The beatings had taken a lot from the old man, though. He forgot things in the middle of a sentence. When a guard called him back to work, he promised Senko that their talk had never happened.

Late in the afternoon Tumbo came down to look at the tunnel and praised the work. Rank 5 handed him his spare pick. Tumbo threw it carelessly, and it stuck point-down in the ground. Up top, Tomon thanked Eronar for the lift. At his word Grisa, a hard woman with a whip at her belt, dragged someone along by the neck, threw them onto the platform and sent it down. Tomon gave Derek, who had been breaking stones all day, ten minutes of rest, and left a guard called Stoyancho to watch the work.

Then everyone went to the celebration.

The Toast

Tumbo lit the fires as the sun went down. The camp was in a good mood. The harpies were gone, and there would be meat and drink.

Eronar stood up and lifted an alchemy jug. He had made it that afternoon with a replicating infusion, and it was filled with enough Fruktoza for every throat in Stonecrack.

"This is a drink from my village," he said. "The best you will ever see."

Tomon had watched him make it with magic and was impressed, and he agreed to keep that a secret. Grisa called every guard over and poured for each of them, and then for herself. Tumbo drank. While the cups went around, Eronar quietly sent a message to Ivak that the drinks were almost ready. The plan had been for two in the morning, but there would be no better moment than this.

The answer came back: "Good, then we're coming. Get ready!"

Tomon poured four cups for the party himself and raised his own.

"Cheers! Let's drink to our new friends, the saviors who let us do our work again!"

He drank last. The four of them only pretended to drink. Melmundo tossed his cup over his shoulder, and Tomon saw it.

"Friend," he said, "you've still got a lot to drink."

He did not get to say more. All around the fires the guards were swaying, their eyes glassy and their hands slack. The Fruktoza had hit them all at once, and it would keep them stunned for a long time.

And over the walls came the spiders.

Webs and Fire

They poured down the palisade like black water. Thick ropes of web shot across the yard and stuck the guards where they stood. Ivak webbed Grisa, Tomon and a bandit beside them. There were six of them, counting Ivak, and one the party never saw.

Melmundo killed a stunned bandit with one blow. Eronar drew his pistol, pressed it nearly to Tomon's head and fired, but Tomon jerked aside and the shot missed. Ivak stepped over the wall and shot two bandits dead. Senko sent a ray of frost into Grisa, and the woman, webbed and furious, glared at her.

Rank 5 moved through the firelight. His dagger found Tomon's throat, and then he was gone into the shadows again. Behind him the spiders were eating the webbed bandits alive.

Tumbo shook off the drink before anyone else. He was still webbed, but he was awake. Melmundo hit him and spat on him, and the big man's face went dark with rage. Ivak spat another web over him and turned to the party.

Fight with us, he told them, or flee.

They chose to fight beside the spiders.

There was fire everywhere in the camp: the celebration fires and the forges. Ivak told Senko to put out the furnace in front of her, and one of his spiders smothered another. Tumbo drew the huge greatsword from his back, roared and swung at Melmundo. The blade hit the ground beside him. Rank 5 came out of the dark again and cut Tomon a second time. Tomon looked very bad now. Melmundo cut across Tumbo's chest.

Ivak looked at Eronar. The spiders would take care of the bandits, he said. He shot one through and leapt onto a furnace.

Tumbo tore the web off himself and swung again, and missed again. Four or five guards had broken free by now and were joining the fight. One of them set a spider alight with a fire arrow, and it burned and later died. A crossbow bolt flew at Ivak's face. He caught it in his hand and snapped it.

Then a bandit up on the wall reached the ballista. It was a huge crossbow, and its bolts were as long as Melmundo was tall. It thumped, and a bolt flew at Ivak. The drider drew his golden sword and cut the bolt in two in the air.

The party shouted for the spiders to seize the man at the ballista. Ivak gave the order. Rank 5 was already aiming his crossbow from the shadows, and the bandit fell dead across his weapon.

Tumbo tried to grab Melmundo with both arms. Melmundo punched him in the belly and broke free. A bolt from a light crossbow went through Senko's shoulder. A bandit leapt from the walls to escape, landed on his neck and did not get up. The spiders came down on the dead and on the hanged harpy and ate.

Rank 5 put an arrow into Tomon from the dark, and the camp leader dropped, out cold. Melmundo drove his sword into Tumbo's shoulder and spat on him again. Ivak wrapped a wounded child of his in web to keep it alive.

Grisa broke out of her web. Her whip cracked across Eronar, and she planted herself over Tomon's body to guard him. Eronar fired at her twice, and twice she dodged.

Then fire began to fall from the sky. Arrows were coming from the forest beyond the walls, burning as they came down, and they killed spiders and a horse at the stable. Ivak roared, took his bow and shot back into the dark.

Tumbo

Tumbo parried Melmundo's next blow and smiled. Half his teeth were missing.

Senko raised her hand toward Grisa and wove a charm around her. The anger went out of the woman's face. She smiled at Senko as if at an old friend, and she would see her that way for the next hour. She ran to Tumbo and told him that these people didn't look so bad.

Tumbo was not listening. He took a small red crystal from his pocket and rubbed it along his blade, and the greatsword burst into flame. He swung it into Melmundo, and Melmundo nearly went down. He was barely standing.

"You will die here!" Tumbo shouted.

Melmundo gulped down a healing potion and struck again. Tumbo caught the sword in his teeth.

"Tumbo! You dog, let go of my sword!"

Eronar fired at him. Tumbo caught the bullet in his teeth as well.

Up on the wall, Ivak had found the ballista smashed to pieces. "Show yourself, coward!" he shouted into the trees.

"What do you see up there?" Eronar called.

"A group! A group of people! They are shooting at us!" There were five of them out in the forest.

Senko's frost missed Tumbo. The big man swung his burning sword into Rank 5's ribs and then back into Melmundo. Light flashed from the medallion of Hikari at Melmundo's chest, and the blow came down at half its strength. It was enough to keep him alive.

Rank 5 stepped in close and drove his dagger into Tumbo's stomach.

Tumbo smiled, closed his eyes and fell. Rank 5 stepped aside and let the body hit the ground.

The Slaves Go Free

Ivak went over the wall and into the far forest. One by one the archers' fires out among the trees went out.

The camp was quiet except for the crackle of burning wood. Rank 5 went to the slave quarters and freed the slaves. Then he went from body to body and collected the bandits' gold, five hundred and eighty pieces in all. Eronar wanted answers from Tomon, so the camp leader was bound in web and carried inside to a chair by the fire.

Derek Fauner came out of the quarters already dressed in his own gear.

Senko brought the cart around, still heaped with harpy feathers, and told the slaves to climb on. They bowed to the party and thanked them. Then they left in pairs on the guards' horses and on the cart, out through the gate and into the night. Grisa, still charmed, complained to Senko that the slaves had all been let go and now someone would have to do the work. Senko patted her on the head.

The Old Dwarf

Eronar sat down with Derek Fauner at last.

The dwarf was very old, with gold rings in his beard. He had been at Stonecrack four months. Bandits had caught him on the road, dragged him here and made him work without rest. Eronar explained that the party had been hired to keep the camp's work going.

"If you respect me," Fauner said, "then you know who I am and what I have done."

He had been on his way to the ruins of Alduin to study what was still hidden there. He had found something.

"Very important information that I must not share here. I would tell you, but not the others."

He was researching the Ermal Citer. Eronar did not speak Elvish, but he knew his history: the name meant "Elemental Crafted." Fauner explained that they were four weapons made long ago by a powerful mage, and each one held its own magic. Each belonged to one of the great elements: fire, earth, water, air. He had found a lead to them in the journal of Omurtak.

He had been going to Selki, he said, to meet his friend Dr. Philip Flane. About the Kartella he knew only that they were criminals. He had heard rumours that Drisko dealt in artifacts, but he could not swear to it.

"An artifact is not an easy thing to find. It is very rare."

Then Eronar told him something he did not tell many people.

"My family was attacked many years ago," he said, "by something that looked like a black panther."

He had lost his mother. He had seen a figure wrapped in dark cloth, and that was all he knew. He came from Kaimol, near Unagura. Fauner thought it over. The closest beast he knew of, he said, was a displacer beast.

Two words came back to Eronar from that night, out of nowhere: Corpus Curse.

Tomon's Last Words

Grisa dragged Tumbo's body across the yard and asked Senko what to do with it. Tumbo went into the pit, and Grisa's body later went in after him. Eronar healed Tomon until his eyes opened.

Rank 5 laid a dagger against his throat.

"For Drisko! I work for Drisko!" Tomon said. "I was sent here thirty years ago!"

Drisko had come a week ago, he said, and ordered the work sped up. He was going to bring a cart full of slaves this week, in two or three days. Tomon's voice cracked.

"He raised me. He saved me. He pulled me, my brother and that dog out of the mud." He wanted to know where the dog was now.

He knew that Drisko dealt in slaves. About artifacts he knew nothing. Drisko had not come alone, either: his daughter Mani had been with him, and one other person. Mani ran an orphanage and looked after children. Rank 5 knew her well. She was the main figure at Don Foster, where a tiefling lived too.

Tomon looked up at the tiefling holding the blade.

"I hope these people kill you slowly."

Then he tore free of the web. A dagger came out of his clothes, and he drove it into Melmundo's belly. The blade was poisoned, and Melmundo crashed to the floor unconscious.

Eronar shot Tomon in the forehead. Rank 5 cut his throat.

In Tomon's pocket Rank 5 found an antidote, and he poured it into Melmundo. The poison left him. Eronar laid his hands on the big man and healed him, and Melmundo opened his eyes. He picked up the poisoned dagger from where Tomon had dropped it.

The Drider's Price

Ivak came back bleeding, and his spider body was on fire. He had hunted down the guards who were left and burned a hidden guard tower.

"Five of my children," he said, "who gave their lives to help you."

Melmundo looked at the golden sword at the drider's side.

"I see this sword would be very useful to me too. What do you want for it?"

Someone dear to Ivak had given him that sword. But he had a job, and he would give it up for that job. A necromancer had taken part of his cave, someone who raised the dead and had fire and an army of spawn.

"I want him dead. I want him out of the cave."

Eronar thought of the skeletons, and of the abandoned temple of Kaguya that stood not far from the cave. The two might be linked. Ivak would help them and hold off some of the undead himself. But the necromancer had to die within a week. The party agreed.

Stonecrack belonged to Ivak and his children now, but he let the party stay at least the night. He spun a thick web down into the pit so they could climb down to the bodies.

"Until we meet again, friends!" he said, and he was gone.

What the Dead Carried

They went through the bodies by firelight.

Tomon had a hundred and fifty gold, eighty silver, a dagger, a fine suit of plate armor, a hat of wizardry and two small blue stones that were cold to the touch. They were runes: struck into a weapon, each would put the bite of frost into it for a minute. Rank 5 kept them. Grisa had thirty gold, ten silver, a shield, a red stone, a light blue stone and a coin stamped with a feather. The red stone was a fire rune and the blue one a lightning rune. Eronar cast a spell over the coin and learned that it was a feather token, which would let whoever held it fall gently. Tumbo had five gold, one silver, another fire rune, greater healing potions and his huge greatsword. Melmundo took the sword.

The hat went to Senko, and the feather token was offered to her too. The plate armor was worth about fifteen hundred gold, and they would keep it to sell. There was too much to carry, so Eronar used his infusion once more and made a Bag of Holding.

Professor Fauner came to say goodbye. Four months in this place had been enough. He invited Eronar and the rest to visit him in Selki and to ask for him or for Dr. Philip Flane. Eronar asked him to speak well of the Compass Gang, and the old dwarf promised he would. Then he rubbed the gold rings in his beard and was gone. Only his footprints showed, walking away across the dirt.

One slave had not left. Dortan stood over Tomon's body, drew a breath and breathed fire over it until there was nothing left but ash. The shackles, he said, had kept his magic down all these years.

The Storm and the Stranger

They slept in the camp. Eronar took a watch.

In the middle of the night the air turned cold. A huge white dragon passed overhead with its long white tail trailing behind it, flying toward the city of Omidzhar. A storm followed it. Rain came down hard on the empty yard.

Dortan stood up in the rain, and his face changed. The broken, forgetful old man was gone. Something serious looked out of his eyes. He asked who they were and why they had done this.

They told him they were the Compass Gang, a group that went looking for adventure.

"I should thank you, then," the voice said.

Then it turned grave. "A great evil fell on Kifi village. An evil that may wipe out the whole continent." He had tried to stop it, he said, and failed.

He looked at Eronar. Did he remember the Corpus Curse?

"You will have to find out who Umbro Hebi is." That was the name of the one who had made the curse called Corpus. They would meet again, the voice said, very soon.

Eronar asked for his name.

"My name is Samuil. Samuil Jack."

The rain kept falling on Stonecrack, and Austin's father stood in it. In the morning the party would have to decide what to do with him.
