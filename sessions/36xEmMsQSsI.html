Chapter 1: Underground Memories

A Letter Signed by Kartella

Dolenizmet had a long memory for war. The continent's old battlefields were grassed over now, its broken fortresses were softened by moss, and the songs about who had burned whom were sung only in the oldest taverns. For a hundred and fifty years the Ao Giri Empire had held the land under one crown. Emperor Ospuh II sat on it now, and most of his subjects had never known anything but peace.

Peace does not mean honesty, though. At the center of the continent, in a camp the imperial roads went around, the criminal brotherhood called Kartella kept its own law. In the camp stood a large timber tavern with a wooden sign above the door that read DRIS Tavern. At its bar sat a man with a pipe. He smoked slowly and watched the door.

His name was Drisko. A week earlier he had sent five letters, all with the same symbol pressed into the wax and all with the same words:

You are invited to the Dris tavern at 19:03. Those who answer will find growth and riches, in gold or in information, whichever they value more.

The letters were signed Kartella Records. Drisko had chosen the recipients carefully. His scouts went everywhere, and they told him who was strong.

The Arrivals

The first to answer came after almost a week on the road. He was a young yuan-ti, slim, with a snake's patient eyes and a flute at his belt. His name was Essail. At the tavern door two bandits stopped him and searched him from collar to boots. Their fingers lingered on the flute as if it might be a blowpipe.

"Nothing personal," one of them said, without much warmth. "We know the boss called some weirdos here."

Inside, a blue dragonborn stood with a scroll of names. His scales caught the lamplight like wet slate. He found Essail's name, ticked it off, and pointed him to a table.

"And you are?" Essail asked.

"It's not important who I am," the dragonborn said, and turned back to the door.

The second guest was a heavy tiefling with shiny black horns and tattoos that showed the initials OL. He called himself Rank 5. He was sent to the same table, sat down across from Essail, and ordered a black beer from a waitress who seemed to resent every order she took.

The third guest had to stoop to get through the door. He was an aasimar so huge that the top of his head nearly scraped the lintel, and he walked in heavily and proudly, as if the floorboards should be grateful to carry him. This was Melmundo. The dragonborn at the door offered him a hand, and Melmundo took it and felt his bones grind together.

"Strong grip," Melmundo said, flexing his fingers afterward.

"Being strong is my job," said the dragonborn. The bandits called him Austin.

At the table the three strangers found that they had at least one thing in common: none of them liked to sit idle. Essail produced three dice, and each of them put one gold coin in the middle. The dice clattered across the wood. Essail rolled fifteen, and he took the three coins with a little bow of the head.

The fourth guest drew laughter before he had crossed the threshold. He looked like an ape: long-armed and broad-chested, with a jungle's wariness in his eyes. One of the guards held out a banana to him, and the others howled. His name was Kollioh. He was a monk, and Drisko's scouts had marked him as strong. He let the joke pass, and they let him in.

The last invited guest was a vedalken: tall, blue-skinned and composed, with the careful look of someone who reads more than he talks. Behind him, light as a shadow, walked a pink-haired kitsune who said nothing at all.

"You are with the Archive?" Austin asked.

"I am," said the vedalken. "I work as an archivist. Eronar."

Austin ran his claw down the scroll and stopped. The kitsune was not on it. At the door one guard muttered to the other, whom he called Stefan, about whether this was allowed. In the end Austin simply wrote a new name at the bottom of the list: Senko. Then he sent them both to the table where the others waited.

Strangers at One Table

Kollioh ordered breakfast, although it was evening. Eronar asked about something on the board called the Fructose.

"Well, the Fructose is only for real men," the waitress said. She explained that it was a shot of many mixed spirits and that not everyone who drank it survived. Eronar ordered one anyway. Then the waitress looked at Senko and curled her lip. She took her for a tabaxi and treated her accordingly, with the kind of contempt that needs no words. Senko did not answer. She rarely did.

They went around the table, and each said a little about himself. Essail said he loved gambling and playing and music, and that he dreamed of travelling with a band one day. Rank 5 said he liked to read books about anime, whatever those might be. Eronar said he liked to investigate and read, and that just now a certain piece of history held his interest. He explained how he had come by his companion: in a forest, the kitsune had stolen his letter. He had got it back, and since then she had followed him wherever he went.

Melmundo studied the silent girl. Then, on a hunch, he spoke to her in Celestial, the tongue of the heavens.

Senko's eyes widened. For a moment it seemed she might answer. Then she looked away and said nothing.

But she was watching, and she had noticed things the others had not. At a nearby table two patrons had gone quiet while the strangers talked. When Celestial was spoken, their heads turned, and their interest sharpened. Senko marked them and kept it to herself.

She noticed something else too. When Rank 5 announced, with great seriousness, that he came from a place called "Tom and Jerry" and was famous there, Senko knew at once that it was a lie. She did not expose him. She only smiled at him, slowly, until he understood that she knew.

The Champion and the Old Man with Animals

A roar went up across the room. A muscular patron named Goshoy had challenged the tavern's drinking champion, a man called Johnny Dolfa. The cups went down one after another. Goshoy turned grey, then green. Then he vomited and fled into the night. Johnny wiped his mouth and raised his arms. It was his twenty-first win in a row, and nobody had ever beaten him.

While the crowd was still cheering, an old man shuffled up to the strangers' table.

He was very old, with a beard that reached almost to his belt, and he did everything slowly: he walked slowly, he blinked slowly, and he spoke slowly, as if every word had to travel a long way to reach his mouth.

"I. Am the animal breeder. Babaduk."

He had three creatures to offer. The first was a blind cave spider that burrowed and climbed, sensed the world through little feelers on its body, and stayed very quiet; its bite, he promised, did a great deal of damage. That one was fifteen gold. The second was a potrifon, a winged blend of griffon and cat with sharp eyes that could see in the dark. It flew faster than either of the others, and it was twenty gold. Where he had found these animals, Babaduk would not say.

For the third he raised one hand. Purple energy gathered in the air and formed a cage, and inside it hovered something like a bee, but larger, brighter and strangely alert. A bazarek, he called it, a fey creature. Its sting carried poison, magic slid off it, and once in a while, when its owner told it whom to help, it could heal. He asked thirty gold.

Eronar leaned closer. His history came back to him from old pages: creatures like this did not come from this world but from the Feywild, and they grew stronger as they grew. One like it was worth three or four thousand gold. Such creatures bonded with a single owner and obeyed only that person.

Essail edged back in his chair. Bees gave him bumps, he said.

Melmundo had no patience for talk of reselling it. They should buy it and keep it, he argued. But between them they did not have thirty gold.

Essail stood up and straightened his coat. He gave one gold back to Melmundo and one to Rank 5. At a table in the corner, a group of old men sat over their dice.

"Oh, of course," he said, when the others raised their eyebrows. "Where there are dice and cards, there I am."

Dice, a Bargain and a Snap of the Fingers

The table belonged to an old man, his wife and two other locals. They welcomed the young yuan-ti the way people welcome a lamb. Essail played politely, then cleverly, then without mercy. When he got up again, he had twenty gold coins in his hand, his own five among them, and the old couple were nearly out of money.

"I am a man who knows how to talk," he explained as he sat down again. "I am a man of art, you understand?"

Then the bargaining began. Babaduk looked at Essail with ancient, unhurried eyes when the yuan-ti tried to suggest a kinship between them.

"Just because we are animals," the old man said, "doesn't mean we are one kind. My snakish friend."

Eronar tried a cleverer approach. He hinted that the creature was not as healthy as it seemed and spoke as though he had heard it complain. Babaduk smiled into his beard.

"Nice try, boy. I know how to talk with such things."

It was Melmundo, in the end, who talked him down: thirty gold became twenty-five. They pooled their coins for it, and the bazarek went to Eronar. He held out his hand, and the creature landed on it and watched him with many-faceted eyes. He named it Buzz4o.

Melmundo looked longer at the potrifon. Babaduk asked twenty for it, then fifteen, and Essail put in five of those. The griffon-cat settled on the aasimar's enormous forearm and folded its wings. It could not speak, but it had eyes that missed very little. Melmundo named it Heritobibubka.

Babaduk counted his coins with great care. Then he raised his hand and snapped his fingers, slowly, and he was gone. Even the cage was gone.

Behind the Bar

A blue-skinned tiefling came to fetch them. His name was Asen. He led them behind the bar, past expensive-looking portraits on the walls, to the man with the pipe.

Drisko looked them over the way a buyer looks over horses. He confirmed that he had sent the letters. Senko he had not invited, but he waved a hand: she could come along too.

The job was simple to describe. A day's travel away stood a ruined old temple. He wanted it searched for anything of value, and above all for one thing. He unrolled a scroll with a sketch on it: a stone charm carved as the face of a cat, with jade set in its left eye. As far as anyone knew, only one was left. The others had been broken, lost or sold through the wars.

"Three hundred gold for the job," Drisko said. "Three hundred more if you bring me the charm."

A month ago he had sent four of his own men to that temple. They had not come back, and he had heard nothing from them since. When one of the party wondered aloud whether the danger matched the pay, Drisko took his pipe from his mouth.

"Boy, what I'll tell you is that people die for fifty gold."

Austin knew where the temple stood, and he would take them there by cart. They were to wait for him at the tavern door early in the morning.

Then Drisko came to his condition. He was not going to pay strangers and then watch them disappear into the forest. He wanted their blood, so that Asen could follow them wherever they went.

Asen produced a small syringe. "Give me your hand, boy."

Essail gave his. Rank 5 gave his and felt the mark settle on him. Eronar gave his. Senko, who knew very well that shadow magic was forbidden in these lands and that blood could open many doors, gave it as well.

Kollioh did not.

"You can't take it from me," he said in his rough Common. "Only in battle."

Six guards moved in around him. There were perhaps thirty-five bandits in the tavern that night, and every one of them seemed to be watching. Austin took the monk by the arm and walked him to a back room. Melmundo went after them to ask what was going on, but the guards stopped him at the door.

Behind it, a single punch knocked Kollioh almost senseless. He stayed on his feet just long enough to sweep his leg at the dragonborn's ankles. Austin turned away from the kick in a cartwheel, landed on top of him and pinned him to the floor.

"Listen to me carefully, monkey," Austin said, close to his ear. "Don't make me end your life."

He drew the blood into a test tube. When they came out again, he tossed the tube across the room, and Asen caught it without looking and put it away.

A Night in the Cartel's House

Asen showed them to two rooms. Drisko sent up two healing potions, and they were given to Eronar to share out. Rank 5 and Essail played one last little game for a single gold coin, and this time the tiefling won. Eronar, with Senko helping him, went over every corner of their room for spying devices or hidden runes and found none.

Heritobibubka had already decided whom it belonged to. It followed Melmundo's every gesture. Buzz4o was slower to trust. It hovered near Eronar but would not obey him yet, and it kept drifting toward Senko, as though it sensed in the fox-girl something of its own kind.

They slept ten hours. Kollioh's bruises faded in the dark, and by morning he was whole again.

Morning

Kollioh woke first; in the jungle he had always risen before the sun. Senko woke nearly as early, as she always had in the forest. She did not bother to call Eronar. She jumped onto his bed, then from his bed onto the next one, and came down squarely on Melmundo's stomach. The aasimar woke with a grunt that shook the shutters.

Eronar tucked Buzz4o into his coat pocket. Melmundo took Heritobibubka outside for a walk in the grey morning air.

Over breakfast they divided the potions. Essail suggested that they go to Kollioh and Melmundo, who would be in the front. Eronar slid one across the table to the monk.

"For you, brother monkey."

Eronar also still had the little glass of Fructose from the night before, sealed and untouched. He examined it now and concluded that it was a fierce spirit, strong enough to paralyse anyone who drank it unlucky. Only a madman would drink it for pleasure. He put it back in his pocket.

A waiter came over, brisk and loud. "So! What'll it be? What do you want?"

Breakfast was five silver each. A day's rations cost twenty-eight silver. Melmundo ordered breakfast and a packed dinner for later. Then he bought three rations for two gold, and Rank 5 quietly gave him some coins. Kollioh had nothing to buy food with, so Essail bought him a day's rations without making a fuss about it.

The Road Through the Forest

Austin had the cart ready at the door. He would not give them money, and he did not offer conversation either. The road ran into a forest so dense that the light came through only in coins and threads, and they rode all day under the branches while the horses snorted at shadows. They ate their rations on the move. Somewhere along the way, in the pocket of Eronar's coat, Buzz4o finally settled. When Eronar spoke to it, it listened, and from then on it did what he asked.

On Tuesday afternoon, a quarter past three by Eronar's reckoning, Austin took the cart down a last hill at a speed that made the wheels slide sideways, and stopped it in a spray of leaves near the ruins.

"One hour," he said. Two of his men climbed onto the driver's bench. "After that they take the cart and go. Drisko doesn't take chances. We lost four good men here a month ago."

The cart rolled away to be hidden. Austin stayed with them.

The Temple of Two Sisters

The temple was very old and mostly collapsed. Its roof had fallen in, and moss grew over the broken stones. Melmundo sent Heritobibubka up. The potrifon flew a wide circle over the ruin, came back to his hand and shrugged its feathered shoulders: it had seen nothing.

Two symbols were carved into what was left of the roof. Eronar and Melmundo knew them both. One belonged to Kagoya, goddess of darkness, shadows and illusion, whom cultists and evil things worshipped. The other belonged to her sister Hikari. Melmundo touched the medallion at his chest, which bore Hikari's holy symbol.

"Since I worship Hikari," he said, "and she doesn't get along well with her sister Kagoya..." He did not finish the thought. He did not need to. Kagoya hated her sister bitterly.

"Good or not," said Eronar, "I don't think we have much choice, overall."

Kollioh climbed onto the broken roof to keep watch. Rank 5 simply vanished. One moment he was there, the next he was a shadow sliding through a doorway.

"Let's not split up," Essail said, a little too late. "What do you say?"

From the roof Kollioh called down. "Directly to the left something reflects the light. We should go there and see."

Inside, Rank 5 had opened a door. Under the gravel and fallen stones his foot found an edge, then a ring. It was a trapdoor.

Then the bones began to move.

The Dead at the Door

Three undead came out of the dark rooms of the temple, with bows on their backs and short swords in their fingers. Essail pulled open a door, and a skeleton stepped out right in front of him. Rank 5 slipped free of the one beside him and fell back. Purple energy rose around Senko and hardened into a shimmering coat of armour, and for once she found her voice and shouted a warning to the others.

"Essail's in danger!" Kollioh roared from above.

Then he jumped. He dropped from the broken roof with his quarterstaff raised and brought it down on a skeleton's skull. The old bones did not resist blunt force well, and the thing burst apart and scattered across the floor.

Another skeleton lunged at Rank 5 with its short sword. The tiefling turned the blade aside with his dagger. When an arrow came at him, he cut it in two in the air and stood his ground in the doorway.

Melmundo's blood was up. Rage filled him like heat filling a forge, and he swung. One skeleton came apart at the waist. Its top half clattered one way and its legs the other.

Two were left. Eronar looked at them, then at Kollioh, and something like a scholar's inspiration crossed his face. He spoke a word, and the monk flew. The spell threw him through the air like a stone from a sling, straight into the last two skeletons. They shattered together. Kollioh got up bleeding but grinning.

Eronar spoke to his pocket. Buzz4o flew out, landed on Kollioh's wounds and left a smear of golden honey on them, and the bleeding stopped.

The Symbol on the Dead

Eronar knelt among the bones. Rags still hung on them, and on the rags was a symbol he knew: the same mark that had been pressed into each of their letters.

Austin looked at it for a long moment. "Those are our four," he said. "Drisko's men. Someone killed them here and raised them up again." He looked into the dark. "A necromancer, maybe."

The dead men had nothing of value on them.

The party spread out to search. Senko and Eronar found a purse with a single gold coin in it, and next to it an oil lantern of a strange, unfamiliar design. Eronar turned it over, felt the magic in it and could not tell what it was for. He took it.

Melmundo had more luck than all of them. In a side room he found an altar black with dried blood, a purse hidden beside it with fifteen gold and twelve silver, and an inscription carved into the stone in a script none of them could read. Eronar copied it carefully into his notes.

Kollioh and Rank 5 found a room of books, burned half to ash. They were poems and stories about the goddess Kagoya. Broken weapons lay among them, and in the debris something small and blue gleamed: a low-grade sapphire. Rank 5 held it up to the light and valued it at about eighty gold.

"I'll pinch it," he said, and it went into his pocket.

Half the Truth

Austin came into the altar room and read the inscription. It was in Undercommon, he said, and it said Goblin. In his opinion goblins had been sacrificed here.

Senko stood in front of the altar and closed her eyes. She could talk with spirits, and the magic in this room still spoke to her. What it told her was worse. Goblins had not been sacrificed here. People had been turned into goblins by a ritual of blood.

She went to Melmundo and spoke quietly in Celestial, which only he understood.

"What he tells us is half the truth. I think we are getting into something very bad."

Melmundo waited until the dragonborn had stepped out. Then he told the others. "They turned people into goblins here. So we have to be careful, guys."

He left Heritobibubka on guard upstairs. Austin heaved the trapdoor open.

The Barrier on the Stairs

Below it a spiral stair of cracked stone wound down into the dark. Bats rustled overhead. Small spiders ran from the light. Halfway down Senko, who could see in the dark, stopped. On a step lay a hand, cut off and perhaps mummified, its fingers curled.

Eronar threw down a stone that he had made to shine. Its light showed a lever in the wall. Then, in mid-fall, the light simply went out, as if it had been snuffed by a wall that no one could see.

Melmundo walked into it. It was like walking into glass. He could go no further.

Eronar studied it and understood: it was a barrier against magic. Anyone who could cast spells and was pushed through it would be stripped of their magic, and anyone who could not would not get through at all. He knew the spell behind it. One day he might learn it himself, if he ever had two hundred and fifty gold to spare. He had twenty-one.

Rank 5 could not reach the lever with his hand, but he could reach it with a lockpick. He hooked it and pulled it down. Four runes flashed blue in the stone around them, and the barrier was gone.

They went on in marching order. The stair went down about a hundred and fifty feet and ended in a passage that stopped at a twenty-foot drop.

The Hall of Red Light

Below lay a huge underground hall. It was ruined, and it was lit from inside the walls by glowing red crystals. In the middle stood an altar, and behind the altar a statue of Kagoya, goddess of shadows and illusion, stood with her face turned toward the dark.

Melmundo's medallion began to glow.

At first it was a faint warmth against his chest. Then it shone brighter and brighter, until the others had to squint, and a little wisp of light rose out of it and hung in the air. It hovered there for a moment, as if deciding. Then the hall around them changed.

They were watching a memory from less than a year ago. The hall stood whole, and a full moon shone down through some opening above. Ghostly worshippers filled it, chanting, and a cultist stood at their head with his arms raised.

"The time is now, my brothers and sisters! This full-moon night will mark a new beginning!"

Six volunteers knelt in the circle of blood: a red tiefling, a drow, a werewolf who was already dead, a vampire, a changeling and a fallen aasimar.

"Tonight," the cultist cried, "we will witness the rebirth of a god. Our shadowy lady Kagoya!"

Something rose out of the circle. It was a shadow, huge and shapeless, taller than the hall. It swept its arms, and the volunteers and worshippers fell. The cultist dropped to his knees and begged for mercy, and there was none. The shadow lifted him up and swallowed him.

Only one ghost was left, a wounded woman. She lowered her head and accepted what was coming. The shadow touched her forehead, and she became a monster.

Then the shadow flew upward, and the temple came down around it.

The wisp drifted back into Melmundo's medallion, and the hall was ruined and red again.

Austin was shaking. The dragonborn who had knocked down Kollioh with one blow, the paladin of Bahamut, the platinum dragon, had gone grey around the eyes. When they asked him, he said that he knew nothing.

The Sarcophagus

They searched the altar. The candles on it were burning, and the lanterns too. Someone had been here recently and had covered their tracks. On the altar lay a yellowish mass. Eronar examined it and felt a chill: it was bone, turned to liquid by force magic.

Beside the altar stood a sarcophagus with no inscription, its lid open a crack. Eronar, Rank 5 and Melmundo put their shoulders to it and pushed together, and the stone lid ground aside. Inside lay a skeleton, a stone charm and the talisman they had come for: a cat's face carved in stone, with jade in its left eye.

The red crystals in the walls, they realised now, were rubies, and valuable ones.

Then they heard feet in the passage.

Mabus Bur

Goblins poured into the hall: soldiers with scimitars and archers with bows. Behind them came a hobgoblin, broad and scarred, with the look of a leader. His name was Mabus Bur.

Eronar raised his pistol and fired, and a goblin soldier dropped dead. Rank 5 cut another goblin in two. A scimitar swung at Melmundo and bounced off a shield of divine light. Austin opened his jaws and breathed lightning across the hall, and an archer was almost burned to ash. When an arrow flew at Senko, the dragonborn's lightning destroyed it in the air.

One archer's arrow missed Rank 5. The other struck Senko hard.

Mabus Bur roared, and a bear tattoo blazed into light across his back. It was a totem blessing, one of only twelve in the world, and it gave him the strength of a beast. Rage filled him, and blades seemed to slide off him. He struck Austin with a blow that should have split him open, and Austin caught it on his guard. He turned Melmundo's swing aside. Austin's sword came down full of holy light. Mabus held his ground, but the radiance burned him.

Senko lifted her hands, and blue fire burst out of them: fox fire. It washed over Mabus, over one of the archers and over Austin too, who stood too close. The hobgoblin howled with smoke rising from his skin.

Then Mabus did something none of them expected. He brought his sword down on the ritual circle on the floor and broke the blood magic.

Red light rose up and wrapped itself around Rank 5 like a cocoon. Inside it he was no longer in the hall. He was seeing through the eyes of the people sacrificed in this place: a tiefling, a werewolf, a vampire, a changeling, a drow elf. He felt what they had felt.

An archer backed away and shot at Senko again. Mabus looked at the fight going against him and turned to run.

Austin raised his hand and spoke a prayer, and the hobgoblin froze where he stood, held fast.

Senko's blue fire found the last archer close to her and burned him down to the skeleton. The other archer looked at his frozen leader, then at the six figures in the red light, and fled into the dark.

Kollioh hit Mabus with his quarterstaff, then spun and finished with a kick. Mabus Bur fell and lay still.

The Hand from Below

Smoke began to rise from Mabus's unconscious body. The floor under him darkened. Then a hand rose out of the ground. It was a hand of shadow, enormous, and it closed around the hobgoblin and dragged him down into the stone. Then the floor was only floor again.

No one spoke for a while.

They took the talisman. Then they tried to dig out the rubies, but every stone that came loose faded and crumbled to red dust in their fingers. Eronar understood too late that they could only be taken with a jeweler's tools.

Austin stood before the altar. Then, with a fury the party had not seen in him even in the back room, he broke it. He smashed the sarcophagus, the altar and the statue of Kagoya until her shadowed face lay in pieces on the floor.

The Road Back

Heritobibubka was waiting upstairs. On the way out Rank 5 scratched its head and took it for a short walk while the cart came back.

Essail was with them on the cart. Down below he had not been. Now, with the forest closing over the road again, he told them why: he had been afraid. Senko watched him while he said it and knew that it was true.

Welcome to the Cartel

They reached the tavern around one o'clock on Wednesday. Drisko received them in his office on the right side of the building, among his own books.

He offered fifty gold for Rank 5's sapphire. Rank 5 kept it. When Drisko began to talk about his lost men and the job, Melmundo leaned forward.

"I already caught you in a lie once. Don't make me do it again."

Drisko took the cat charm and paid six hundred gold for it. For the strange lantern he paid fifty gold to each of them. Then he leaned back, drew on his pipe and studied them.

"How about you work directly for me? You will become my agents."

There was work already waiting. In the last few days harpies had been attacking one of his camps, where iron ore was processed. They would deal with that first. After that he would send them to the ruins of Alduin, because the talisman they had brought him was only one part of something larger, and its other part lay there.

He told them, almost casually, that he had sacrificed twenty-five people to get the talisman. Then he spread his hands. The tavern was their home now.

"Welcome to the cartel."

A Warning

Austin caught them in the common room afterward, among the noise of the bandits. The light was low, and the dragonborn kept his voice lower.

He warned them not to trust anyone in this tavern, and Drisko least of all.

Then he went back to his place by the door, where he checked the names of those who came in, and the party was left at their table with Drisko's gold and the blood in Asen's keeping.
