Chapter 7: Technical Difficulties

A Merchant Made of Air

They had left a lot behind them in Selki. The ankhegs were dead, and the farmers there could walk their fields again. Captain Teor had pressed a new dagger into Rank 5's hand. The Compass Gang had a new companion as well: Q, a paladin of the goddess Shalira, who had fought the burrowing beasts beside them and had not left since.

They had also lost Eronar.

It had happened over a game of cards, a deck they had bought from the goblin trader Ardeo. Eronar and Rank 5 had each drawn a card. Then the Vedalken was gone. His clothes, his tools and everything he carried lay in a heap where he had sat. Rank 5's card had given him something else: the knowledge of where his friend had gone. Eronar was somewhere in Pueven, the cave above Stonecrack, where a necromancer had made his home.

So in the early morning the cart rolled north again, back along the road they had hoped not to see so soon.

Melmundo drove. The reins looked like thread in his hands. He was a very tall Aasimar, and the bench groaned when he shifted, and the horse seemed to know who held it. The air was cold and smelled of wet grass. Mist lay in the low places, and the sky over the hills was the colour of pewter.

Someone tapped Melmundo on the shoulder.

He turned. A goblin sat on the cart behind him, small and green and grinning, with a pack on his back.

"Ardeo," Melmundo said.

The goblin spread his hands as if a shop were opening between them. He had things to sell, he said. Good things, useful things, things a group heading into a cave would want.

How had he got onto a moving cart in the middle of nowhere? Ardeo only tapped the side of his nose.

"A secret."

He reached out to clap Melmundo on the arm, and his hand passed straight through it. For a heartbeat the goblin looked at his own fingers inside the barbarian's sleeve. Then the image thinned like breath on a cold window and was gone. There had never been anyone there but the four of them, the horse and the road.

Nobody said much after that. The cart rolled on.

Rank 5 had been watching Q's hands for a while. On one finger the paladin wore a gold ring. The tiefling knew what it was, and he wanted it badly.

"The Ring of Jumping," he said. "Give it to me. I love jumping."

Q raised an eyebrow.

"I was the best gymnast in Tom and Jerry," Rank 5 said, as if that settled it. "We're famous for our acrobatics."

Q considered him for a moment, then slid the ring off and handed it over. Rank 5 put it on at once and turned his hand in the grey light to admire it. It would be an hour before the ring's magic forgot Q and learned him instead, but he wore it as if it had always been his.

Senko wanted to see the road ahead. She was small, and the cart's sides were high, so Melmundo simply lifted her by the waist and held her up like a child at a fair. She looked left and right, her fox's ears turning. The road was empty. It was not a main road and few people used it. Nothing moved on it but the wind in the hedges.

While she looked, Rank 5 was giving out gifts. From his coat he took small vials of dark liquid, Assassin's Blood, a poison he had bought for the work ahead. He gave two to Melmundo and one to Q. Then he gave the paladin a potion as well.

"Enlarge," he said. "It makes you bigger. A goblin called Arnold told me it's good for whoever stands in front." He looked Q up and down, the plate armor and the shield. "That's you."

Widow's Nest

They knew Stonecrack before they saw it, because the trees began to change.

First there was a thread or two of white between the branches, catching the light. Then whole sheets of it, then trunks wrapped from root to crown so thickly that the bark could not be seen. The webs drifted in the wind and pulled at the leaves. By the time the camp came into view, the old palisade and the barracks were draped like furniture in a house nobody lived in.

The sign was gone. The board that had once named the slave camp of Drisko and Kartella had been taken down, or eaten, or buried under silk.

A small spider sat on a strand near the gate. When it saw them it plucked the web, once and again, in a rhythm. The thread trembled away into the camp, and somewhere deeper other threads answered. A message was going round.

A young spider crept out along a beam, looked at Rank 5 and shrank back. It did not know him. The older ones did, and they kept their distance with something like respect.

Then the web overhead bulged and broke, and Ivak came down.

The drider turned a full circle in the air, legs folded in, and landed in front of them with a thud that shook dust from the nearest roof. He straightened, the human half of him rising tall above the spider body, and spread his arms.

"This is my new home," he said. "I call it Widow's Nest."

He seemed pleased with it, and with himself. He had had visitors, he told them. People with notebooks and questions had come up the road and asked who he was, what he was, and what he was doing here. Journalists. He had scared them off. Their birds had come later anyway and flown over the camp, dropping newspapers everywhere like leaves. He had torn them all up except one, which he had kept for the Compass Gang.

He gave it to them. The paper was creased and smelled of damp. The headline across the top read The Battle for Power in Jerry.

Rank 5 read it twice. Jerry was his city. Tom and Jerry was his guild. Whatever was happening there, it was happening without him.

Melmundo asked about the sword.

Ivak's eyes went to the golden blade, the one that had once belonged to Captain Teor. "When my help is repaid," the drider said, "the sword will be yours."

Rank 5 leaned close to Melmundo, his voice low. "We could kill him and take his sword, bro. Skip all of this."

Melmundo did not even turn his head. "He is our partner and our friend. Don't even think about such things."

The tiefling shrugged, as if he had only been talking about the weather.

Ivak was looking over the group. Something was wrong with the count, and he found it.

"Where is the blue boy?"

Melmundo told him. Eronar had vanished, and he was in Pueven cave, in the necromancer's lair. They had to get him out.

Ivak nodded slowly. That suited him, he said, because he had business in that cave too. The landslide that had shaken these hills had destroyed the lair of a spider brood mother and hurt her badly. He had promised her his old lair, the one in Pueven. But the cave was full of the dead now, walking and crawling and lying in wait. He wanted every undead thing in it gone before he would hand it over to her.

He would bring help. Two mature spiders came down out of the webs, large as ponies, and three medium ones, each the size of a big dog. Ivak saw the party's faces.

"They will not touch you," he said. "You have my word."

Melmundo put a hand on Q's shoulder and introduced the paladin as the newest friend of the group.

"I am Q," said the paladin, and Ivak inclined his head.

A Horse Dead from the Inside

The web was the road now. Ivak's children had strung thick cables of silk from the camp up the slope to the hill above, where the harpies had once nested. The party climbed them, hand over hand and foot over foot. The spiders flowed past them on all sides with insulting ease.

At the top the old nest was a ruin of sticks and feathers, as they had left it. Something new lay among the wreckage.

It was a horse. It had been dead a day, perhaps two. It lay on its side, and there was hardly a mark on the outside of it. But the belly had a wrongness to it, a hollow slackness, as if something had taken it apart from within. Under its left thigh there were burns, dark and blistered, not the kind a campfire leaves.

Ivak crouched over it and sniffed.

"Definitely the work of some necromancer."

He stood back and made a small gesture to his children, and the spiders closed in on the carcass. The party looked elsewhere while they ate.

The Dead Below the Stairs

The mouth of Pueven was dark and breathed cold air at them. Ivak stopped at the threshold. He looked at the ground for a long time, the way a hunter reads a trail.

"Someone has been here," he said. "Recently. And they covered their tracks."

He sent the party in first. He and his children would guard the rear. His eyes went to Q as he said it. A paladin was the right blade for the dead.

Q went a few steps into the dark and stood still with his eyes half closed, feeling for something the others could not. When he opened them his face was grim. There were undead here, many of them. Most of them were to the left.

Senko's eyes caught something before anyone else's did: a shape on the floor ahead that was not rock. It was a human body, stiff and pale in the dark.

Q raised his hand and spoke a prayer to Shalira. A warmth settled on Melmundo, on Rank 5 and on Q himself, a feeling that the next blow would land true.

The passage led to a set of stairs going down beside an old trapdoor. Melmundo was the first to reach them.

The corpse rose.

It came up off the steps all at once. It was a dead man with grey skin and slack jaw, and it hit Melmundo across the face with an open hand, a heavy, wet slap. The barbarian staggered. Then he grabbed the arm that had struck him and tore it off at the shoulder.

The zombie did not seem to notice. It came at him again. Q's sword flashed between them and turned the blow aside. A moment later the zombie lost its other arm, and still it would not stop. A strike that should have finished it went through it, and it swayed and stayed on its feet out of sheer stubborn death.

Behind it the bones on the floor began to move.

They pulled themselves together out of the litter: ribs finding spines, skulls rolling onto necks. Skeletons stood up with shortbows and shortswords in their hands and empty sockets turned toward the living.

Rank 5 slid into the shadows and came out of them behind a skeleton. One strike, placed where a man's heart would be, and the thing burst apart into a rain of bones.

One of the others drew and loosed at Rank 5. The tiefling's dagger moved faster than the eye could follow and cut the arrow in two in the air. Another charged Senko and its rusty blade bit into her.

Then Senko stepped forward with blood on her robe. Light gathered around her: pale foxfire, blue at the edges and white at the heart. It broke over the stairs in a wave. The zombie, armless and stubborn, was simply splattered across the wall. The skeletons were thrown back fifteen feet, rattling.

Melmundo's rage came up in him like a fire catching. He stopped caring about his own guard and swung with everything he had, and another skeleton went to pieces against the wall.

Q raised his hand and loosed a bolt of fire. It went wide, swerved upward and splashed harmlessly against the ceiling. The paladin glared at it.

One was still standing. Rank 5 took a breath, crouched, and went. He crossed the distance in a single blur with a dagger in each hand. When he stopped on the far side the skeleton stood a moment longer. Then it came apart, cut clean in several places at once.

The cave was quiet again.

Behind them Ivak's legs clicked on the stone. "Impressive," the drider said.

Melmundo pressed his hands to his own wounds, and a soft light came from his palms and closed them.

Runes in the Dark

Further in, the webs began. They were old and grey and familiar, and Ivak's whole body changed as he saw them. This had been his place.

"We must kill all the undead here," he said. "I cannot leave the brood mother to deal with them."

Senko stopped them with a raised hand. Across the passage, low to the floor, a string had been drawn tight. At either end of it a stone sat against the wall, and on each stone a green rune glowed faintly. She knelt close without touching anything and studied the marks. She knew their shape. They were poison runes, laid as a trap. A foot on the string would set them off.

They went back a good distance. Someone took aim with a crossbow, and the bolt struck the string. The runes flared. A green poison breathed out of them and filled the passage, thick and stinking, and then it slowly thinned away. When it was gone the runes had erased themselves from the stones.

They went forward feeling clever. A few steps on, a yellow rune they had not seen woke on the wall.

Lightning came out of it in crackling bolts. It struck Q, ringing off his plate, and struck Senko, who cried out. Then the rune dimmed and began to glow again, slowly, like an eye opening. It was recharging.

Senko watched it with her teeth gritted, counting where the bolts had gone and where they had not. The light did not reach everywhere. On its right side there was a blind spot, a strip of passage the rune could not see. They went along it one at a time, hugging the stone.

It was not the last of them. Deeper in, another rune trap caught three of them at once with lightning and with bolts that pierced like iron. Ivak pushed forward and spat a thick wad of web over the glowing marks.

"Hurry," he said.

They hurried. The rune under the web was already cracked and failing, and between them they broke it.

Ivak moved ahead with his children after that, and the party came to a room that smelled worse than anything yet. There were bodies here, half eaten. In the middle of the floor sat a pressure plate, and on it a red rune.

Rank 5 crouched in the doorway and looked at it. Then he looked at the shadows around it. He nocked an arrow and shot the rune from where he stood.

Stone ground on stone. Off to the side of the room, a pit they had not noticed came alive. Three dead things had been hiding in it, zombies and a skeleton, waiting for living feet on the plate. A boulder came down from above and slammed into the mouth of the pit, sealing them inside. They heard scratching behind the rock, and then nothing.

If anyone had stepped on the plate, the dead would have come up out of the pit, and the boulder would have shut the way out behind them.

Ivak's Old Lair

Rank 5 put his head into the next chamber and let his eyes adjust. He could see in the dark. There was nothing worth seeing. This had been Ivak's web lair, and it was full of bones. Broken armor lay among them, and broken weapons too twisted and rusted for any smith to save. There was nothing to take.

"I know this cave best," Ivak said. "I will lead."

Nobody argued.

The young spiders had changed toward the party. They did not shy away any longer, and one of the smaller ones grew bold enough to scuttle right up to them, curious. Ivak shot a line of web at it, reeled it in and stuck it on his own back. It sat there in disgrace for the rest of the walk.

The passage opened out into a wide space. Ivak stopped at its edge.

"Pueven has eighty-five tunnels," he said. "They all join here." He pointed with one long leg. "Watch the ground. There are holes."

There were craters, and they were not shallow. Rank 5 leaned over the lip of one and stared down with his darkvision. The dark went on and on below him, and he could not see the bottom.

When they stopped, Melmundo saw that Senko was bleeding badly. The lightning and the skeleton's blade had both left their marks. He held out a healing potion to her.

She looked at it as if he had insulted her mother, and did not take it.

On the far side of the meeting place, a tunnel led on. It was narrow, far too narrow for a drider and his brood.

"You go ahead," Ivak said. "We will use the other tunnels to go around."

He and his spiders climbed into another opening and were gone. Senko went into the narrow way first. Melmundo helped her up into it, and the others followed.

The Chasm

The tunnel ended at nothing.

The floor simply stopped. In front of them the cave had torn itself open. It was a great pit left by the landslide, so wide that their light did not reach the far side at first, and so deep that it might as well have had no bottom. Pillars of rock stood up out of the dark here and there across it, tall stalagmites with their tips standing above the edge, spaced like stepping stones laid by a giant who did not care whether anyone could reach the next one.

They stood a long time looking at it.

Q turned the problem over. He had given Rank 5 the Ring of Jumping, but its magic could still carry one of them a long way, once, for a minute, before it needed rest. Someone had to get across first and make it easy for the rest.

They pulled out their ropes, one of silk and one of hemp, and knotted them together. Two good ropes, well tied, would hold anyone.

The rope needed an anchor on this side. Melmundo had a spare longsword he never used. They drove it into the rock and hammered it until it would not move, then tied the rope to its hilt.

Rank 5 took the free end. He flexed the hand with the ring on it and grinned at no one in particular.

Then he jumped.

He went from stalagmite to stalagmite like a cat crossing rooftops. Each leap was longer than a man should manage, and each landing was on a tip of rock no wider than a table. On one landing his foot slid. For a moment there was nothing under him but the black, and the others held their breath. His fingers found the rock and closed on it. He pulled himself up and went on.

On the far side he tied the rope fast to a stalagmite and pulled it taut. Now there was a line across the dark.

Senko did not use the rope. She gathered herself and shrank, and where she had stood there was a small fox, bright-eyed and glowing. Fire trailed from her fur as she ran to the edge. She jumped, and she was a spark crossing the night, and Rank 5 reached out and caught her against his chest.

Q was next, and Q was a problem. He wore plate, the heaviest armor there is. No one wanted to hang that weight on a rope over a bottomless pit.

"My armor is special," he said. "It comes off at once."

He snapped his fingers, and the armor was not there any more.

He started across. Halfway, something went wrong, a slip or a misjudged reach, and he dropped. At the last instant he drove his sword into the side of a stalagmite and hung from it. His boots kicked over the dark. Slowly, with his teeth bared, he hauled himself back up.

When he reached the far side and stood in the light, they saw him properly for the first time. Without the steel he was a half-elf, fair-skinned and blond. He was younger than the armor had made him look.

Melmundo came last, and he was the heaviest of them all. He nearly fell too. He threw his arms around a stalagmite as he lost his footing, and the stone began to crack under his weight with a sound like ice breaking. He did not wait for it to finish. He jumped from the breaking pillar and landed among the others, and behind him a piece of the stalagmite tumbled away into the dark. They never heard it hit the bottom.

Senko looked at the scrapes on him and at the blood still drying on herself. When healing was offered again, she refused it again.

The Necromancer

What waited beyond the chasm came quickly, and it was terrible. They found the necromancer. They found Eronar, too, and Eronar woke to a sight he would not forget: Senko on the ground, bleeding, with a dagger wound in her stomach, and the necromancer standing over her. There was a fight. Rank 5 drew the man's blood. There were children in that place, or things that looked like children, and fire. One of them burned.

When it was over, the necromancer was still alive, and so were they. He sat in the dark with them, wounded, and his name was Abadon.

He was a fallen Aasimar. Whatever light had once been in him had gone wrong. One of the party spoke to him sharply. He had learned another lesson today, they told him, and if no one had helped him he would not be among them any more. Abadon blamed himself for a whole city he had failed to save. He was told he could not have saved it alone. He was not to blame.

"I'm already dead anyway," Abadon said. "And so are they."

"A group like ours," Melmundo said, "can't be led by just anyone."

Abadon looked at him for a long moment. "Here I agree with you, Melmundo."

That was when Ivak came back.

The drider came out of a side tunnel without a sound. He walked up to Abadon and set one huge leg down on the stone beside the necromancer's head. Its tip was a hand's breadth from his ear.

"You are very lucky, boy," Ivak said, "that you know these people." Otherwise, he made clear, he would have filled Abadon's body with spiders.

Then he took his golden sword, the one that had once been Teor's, and gave it to Melmundo.

Then his eyes moved to Rank 5, or rather to Rank 5's belt.

"This dagger you carry. Where did you get it?"

Rank 5 turned to Eronar and asked the name of the village, and then told the drider. "We were in the village of Kyutyuk and met your old friend. He said you two were together before."

Captain Teor. In Selki, which some called Kyutyuk. Long ago Ivak had given that dagger to Teor. Teor had given it to Rank 5 for this mission, they explained, so that something of him would be there, as if he were fighting beside them. He had wanted to come himself, but he could not leave his post guarding the village.

Ivak was quiet for a long while. He looked from the sword in Melmundo's hands to the dagger on Rank 5's belt.

"It seems our time is over," he said, "and it is time for another generation. So be it." He straightened. "Carry the weapons with honour. May they serve you well."

There was something more, and it cost him to say it. He was ending their partnership.

"Only one of my five children is still alive. I cannot afford to lose more."

He gave them a message for the captain in Kyutyuk. If Teor ever wanted to visit, he knew where Ivak was.

"You also know where I am," Ivak told them. "And I will never refuse you."

Someone asked how he would even know if they needed him.

"I have my methods."

He turned his eyes on the new boy, Abadon, and looked him up and down slowly from head to foot. Abadon did not look back at him. Then Ivak raised a hand to all of them in farewell and went back into the tunnels, and the clicking of his legs faded into the stone.

A Plan, a Link and an Oath

Abadon spoke to Melmundo as if no one else were there.

He had been on his way somewhere before all this. There was a cave in the Dris mountains where he was to meet an acquaintance, someone who also stood against Akul. And Akul was nothing small. Abadon described a huge monster that commanded shadow magic on a scale he had never seen. It was surrounded by some of the strongest people he had ever laid eyes on, and by thousands of mutated creatures.

Melmundo did not hesitate. He would always help him fight, he said.

"I have no problem with that, as long as I am with you, Abadon."

Abadon almost laughed. "This is not your fight. Friend, I tried to kill you a few moments ago."

Melmundo did not take back what he had said.

Abadon's eyes went black, whites and all, like ink spilled into water. Something reached out from him and touched Melmundo's mind. It did not take hold. It left a door. Once a day, for as long as ten minutes, they would be able to speak to each other through it, wherever they were.

Then Abadon turned to Eronar, and the air changed.

Who had sent him? Why had he appeared in this cave? Abadon wanted an answer.

Eronar did not give him one. He was pale and hollow-eyed, and his voice was very steady.

"Your death will come from my hand. I don't know when. I don't know where."

He had woken up, he said, to see Senko bleeding and Abadon standing over her. Abadon was alive now only because of some debt he felt he owed Melmundo. Abadon did not deny it. Years ago, he said, he had sworn revenge on everyone who had wronged him, and Melmundo had been one of them. Things were different now.

"You are arrogant, small, annoying, and blind to everything that happens in the world," Abadon told Eronar.

Eronar said he did not know whether this world deserved to exist at all. Then he hit Abadon in the face. Abadon let him.

"I want this world to stop existing," Eronar said, "and it will start with you."

Abadon drew a sword and drove it through his own palm. Blood ran down his wrist, dark in the low light. He held out the wounded hand. Eronar took it. They shook on it: a duel to the death, one day, between the two of them.

No one saw what Eronar's other hand was doing while they shook. Under cover of the grip he held an empty vial beneath their joined hands. When they let go, it was full of Abadon's blood, and it was already in his pocket.

The Bonfire

Abadon spoke a word, and a bonfire sprang up out of the bare stone. He sat down with his back against the wall and held his hands out to the flames.

In that light, they could see that the right one was not his.

It was sewn on at the wrist. The stitches were dark and ran with something that was not quite thread. The skin of it did not match his own.

"I took the life of a lieutenant of the Wolves," he said. "This is his hand."

The Wolves were the empire's law in this region, and its hunters. Abadon talked about shadow magic the way another man might talk about an old injury. He had not been born to it. He had learned it the hard way, over years.

"Shadow magic is forbidden in the empire, boy." The Wolves would hunt anyone they knew practised it.

His black eyes found Senko across the fire. He had known what she was from the first.

"A shadow mage always recognizes another."

He had nearly killed her today, a dagger in the stomach, and they both knew it. Perhaps that was why he did what he did next. He took a scarf from somewhere about him. Shadows poured off its edges like smoke and pooled around his wrist. He gave it to Melmundo.

"It will serve her better," he said, with a nod toward Senko.

Melmundo passed it on, and after a moment Senko took it.

What the Blood Was

Later, away from the fire, Eronar took out the vial and turned his magic on it to see what it really was.

The blood was corrupt. It was fallen-Aasimar blood, rotten through with shadow magic, and the corruption was slowly killing the man it came from. Made into a poison, it would kill whoever took it within a day. Only one spell could stop it: Greater Restoration.

The Child

Q had been quiet since the fight. Now he was not.

"You burned a child?"

The question hung over them. Rank 5 answered first. It had been an accident, he said. He had been trying to hit a clone, and it had dodged. And then, more coldly: those had not been children. They had been shadow priests. He had no regrets.

Q let it go, though not easily. He turned to Senko instead and asked her what she knew about Maverick, the man they had met in the tavern.

She had met him once, Senko said, and she had not liked him.

Eronar Drinks

Eronar took the vial out again, and this time he did not study it.

He drank it.

His heart began to race, hard enough that the others could almost hear it. His face went slack, and he fell where he stood. The glass broke beside him on the stone.

They were on him at once. His skin was hot, far too hot, as if a fire had been lit inside him. He did not wake.

Melmundo got him up across his back and carried him up out of the low place to where Abadon sat. The necromancer looked at the limp Vedalken. Then he bent and picked up the pieces of the broken vial and smelled them, and his mouth thinned. He walked to the edge of the drop, where the cave fell away seventy feet into the dark, and threw the shards over.

"My advice to you is to kill him," Abadon said. "He is no longer useful to you."

Only Greater Restoration could cure what Eronar had done to himself. Nothing else would.

Melmundo grabbed him by the front of his clothes. Who could cast it? Where could they find someone who could?

Abadon did not struggle. He laid two fingers against Melmundo's stomach, lightly, just where a blade would go in. The threat in it did not need words. Melmundo held on anyway, and after a moment Abadon gave him what he could. If Eronar was still alive after eight hours, that was a good sign. They should take him to a city. Or to Mrats, the nearest village.

He was going on to the Dris mountains. Melmundo had promised to fight beside him. Now Melmundo stood with Eronar's heat burning through the back of his shirt and made his choice. He would save Eronar. He asked Abadon to tell him, through the link, whether he survived what was coming.

"If you're going to play the hero," Abadon said, "do it without me. You know where I am."

He gathered the last of his strength. The shadows around him thickened and folded in on themselves, and when they unfolded he was not there.

The Road to Mrats

They drove for a whole day.

Melmundo had the reins again. Eronar lay in the back of the cart under a blanket he did not need, hot as a stove, while the others took turns watching his breathing. The road was long and grey, and the wheels found every rut in it. Nobody talked much.

Rank 5 was hearing things.

It had started in the morning, and it did not stop. It was not the cart, the wind or the horse. It was something under those sounds and inside his own head. Voices, or one voice, murmuring things he could not quite make out and did not like the shape of. He shook his head now and then, as a man does at a fly. It did not help.

That night he slept, and he dreamed.

In the dream he was being praised. Faces turned toward him, voices said his name, and gold fell around him in a shining rain. He held out his hands for it and it piled up around his feet, and then his knees, warm and heavy. Then it was not gold any more. It was red and it was wet, and it was rising. It was blood, a pool of it, and he was in it up to his chest and his chin. Then he was under it, drowning.

He remembered something then, as if the blood had washed it up. It was the temple of Kaguya and the room beneath it, where the ritual had been. The cultists had let drops of their own blood fall to wake it, and he had stepped onto the portal at its heart.

There was a woman in the dark with him. She wore a long red robe.

"My name is Mortisha."

She had been there, she told him, a year ago, at that ritual. She had survived it. When the shadows came to swallow her she had prayed to the god Chakram, the lord of lies and betrayal, of power and war and blood, and Chakram had heard. She had been watching ever since, through Rank 5's eyes. She had seen all of them.

She wanted revenge. She wanted it on the great shadow that had answered the ritual. She wanted it on a girl who had woken her and filled her head with sweet words.

"You are like me," she said. "Chosen by Chakram." And he was not the only one among his companions who had felt the pull of that god. Someone else in his group was seeking Chakram's power too.

She wanted to use him. Her offer was power, and the price was the body he stood in.

"Your body will be mine," she said.

Rank 5 refused. He had friends, he told her. They cared for him.

She smiled at that. Over the coming days, she said, she would corrupt his body bit by bit and take his place. Then she showed him what that would mean. She could kill Q through his hands.

"Will Melmundo accept," she asked softly, "his best friend driving a dagger through his throat for no reason?"

"Now get out of my head."

She did not get out. She came in.

The red robe folded around him and through him, and she sank into him like dye into cloth. He felt his body change. Beneath his eyes marks were drawn on his skin in a dark red, like old blood. With them came knowledge, sudden and whole, as though he had always had it. He knew how to take hold of the blood in another body and command it. He knew how to wrap his fist in a gauntlet of gore.

When he woke, the cart was still rolling toward Mrats. Eronar was still burning in the back of it. All of them had come out of Pueven stronger than they had gone in.

Rank 5 lay still and listened. The voice was quiet now. He knew it was still there.
